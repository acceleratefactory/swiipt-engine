And this is the key distinction I would preserve from the work we did on Postpartum:

“Nigeria → Canada” is not the niche.

It is a filter.

“First 90 days” is not necessarily the niche.

It is a life stage.

“Banking” is not necessarily the niche.

It is a problem domain.

The real unit we're hunting is:

Nigeria → Canada → family with children → first 90 days → first major household crisis → scattered advice doesn't solve it → fear that they cannot make the new life work → a functioning, confident household in the new country.

Or:

Nigeria → UK → professionally established wife → trailing spouse → cannot immediately work → loses independence and identity → feels she sacrificed her life for the family's migration → rebuilds personal agency in the new country.

Or:

Nigeria → Gulf → married father → provider for extended family → migration increases his own household costs → family back home continues requesting money → guilt prevents boundaries → creates a sustainable two-household support system.

That is the level of specificity I would keep pushing toward.

And importantly, I would not choose the winner yet. We should first map the full market universe, then research these customer states for frequency, intensity, failed alternatives, willingness to pay, existing solutions, cultural specificity, and transformation potential—exactly the discipline we've been applying to the other life-area markets.
