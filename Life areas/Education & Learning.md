BUT I THINK THE REAL GOLD IS EVEN DEEPER

When I look at this domain through the same lens we used for Postpartum, I see five particularly powerful transformation families:

1. “My child is falling behind.”

Academic recovery.

2. “My child should be able to do this without me.”

Learner independence.

3. “I don't understand how to navigate this educational system.”

Parent/school/curriculum navigation.

4. “Our family's education has become impossible to manage.”

Family education operations.

5. “I keep learning but nothing changes.”

Adult learning → competence.

Those are considerably more interesting than generic “education.”

And there is a sixth one I would watch very closely:

🔥 “Life changed, and education got disrupted.”

That produces hidden markets around:

migration → school transition
divorce → two-household education
financial distress → education continuity
illness/family crisis → academic recovery
homeschool → formal school
school failure → alternative education
career transition → adult reskilling

That is exactly the kind of specific life-state + trigger + failed attempt + emotional stake structure that can produce a deep transformation library rather than a collection of generic education ebooks.

For this domain, I would therefore not let the research team stop at “student” or “parent.” Every promising branch should keep drilling until we can literally say:

“This is a [specific person] whose [specific educational situation] has changed because of [specific trigger], they tried [specific failed solution], but [specific constraint] prevents it, and they desperately want [specific measurable transformation].”
