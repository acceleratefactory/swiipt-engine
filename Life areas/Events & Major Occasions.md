Not:

“Nigerian wedding planning.”

Not:

“Funeral planning.”

But:

Adult daughter → mother dies → she becomes the person everyone expects to coordinate the burial → she is simultaneously grieving, answering relatives, managing vendors and handling money → she cannot emotionally process the death → move from grief-driven chaos to structured family coordination while preserving space to grieve.

Not:

“Event budgeting.”

But:

Parent → paying for child's wedding → relatives keep adding expectations → every “small” addition feels socially impossible to reject → budget keeps expanding → parent fears damaging retirement savings but feels obligated to provide a respectable celebration → controlled, dignified wedding without financial self-destruction.

Not:

“Guest management.”

But:

Nigerian family → 60 relatives travelling from different cities for a wedding → multiple arrival times, rooms, children, elderly guests and transport requirements → WhatsApp coordination collapses → host spends the entire wedding weekend solving logistics → coordinated family hospitality without becoming the unofficial hotel manager.

And not:

“Owambe.”

But:

Working Nigerian adult → receives 12 wedding/funeral/birthday invitations in one month → each carries an expected contribution, outfit, transport or gift → saying no creates guilt/social consequences → salary cannot support the social calendar → participate meaningfully without allowing social obligations to consume the household budget.

That is the hidden-market level I would use for this entire category.
