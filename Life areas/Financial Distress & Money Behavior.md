01 — MONEY AVOIDANCE

This is one of the most important hidden markets.

The person isn't necessarily incapable of budgeting.

They avoid looking at the numbers.

Situations
avoids checking bank balance
avoids opening banking app
ignores bills
doesn't know total debt
doesn't know monthly spending
avoids financial statements
postpones financial decisions
doesn't open financial emails
leaves bills until reminders arrive
Specific person

A person whose finances have become emotionally threatening enough that not knowing feels psychologically easier than knowing.

Trigger

A bill, overdraft alert, debt reminder, bank statement.

Failed attempt

“I'll sit down this weekend and sort everything out.”

They never do.

Constraint

Looking at the numbers creates anxiety/shame.

Emotional stake

“I'm afraid to find out how bad things actually are.”

Desired transformation

Avoidance → safe financial visibility → actionable next step.

This is a very strong market because the person doesn't need another spreadsheet.

They need a way to face money without becoming overwhelmed.

02 — FINANCIAL PARALYSIS

Transformation

NOW LET'S GO DEEPER INTO SPECIFIC MONEY BEHAVIOR

The really valuable unit is this:

Trigger → Behavior → Immediate Reward → Delayed Cost

For example:

Stress spending

Stress → online shopping → emotional relief → financial regret

Family giving

Relative asks → send money → relief/guilt reduction → personal cash shortage

Debt avoidance

Debt notification → don't open → temporary emotional relief → larger financial problem

Payday spending

Salary arrives → spending spree → feeling of abundance → month-end shortage

Savings withdrawal

Unexpected expense → withdraw savings → immediate relief → no emergency buffer

This behavioral chain is much more commercially useful than simply labeling someone an “impulse spender.”

Example 1 — Money avoidance

→ Trigger: Bank/debt reminder arrives.

→ Failed attempt / constraint: They keep telling themselves they'll “sort everything out this weekend,” but looking at the total problem causes anxiety.

→ Emotional stake: “I'm scared that if I look properly, I'll discover I'm much worse off than I thought.”

→ Desired transformation: Avoidance → clear financial picture → prioritized recovery plan.

Example 2 — Payday cycle

Broad topic: Financial Distress & Money Behavior

→ Submarket: Paycheck-to-paycheck cycle

→ Specific life situation: Salary arrives and disappears within days.

→ Specific person: Salaried parent supporting a household.

→ Trigger: Payday.

→ Failed attempt / constraint: They create monthly budgets but abandon them after unexpected expenses and family requests.

→ Emotional stake: “I work every month but still feel financially unsafe.”

→ Desired transformation: Payday reaction → deliberate cash-flow allocation → money that lasts through the month.

Example 3 — Emotional spending

Broad topic: Financial Distress & Money Behavior

→ Submarket: Stress spending

→ Specific life situation: Person spends online after exhausting workdays.

→ Specific person: Overworked professional with discretionary income.

→ Trigger: Stress + evening loneliness.

→ Failed attempt: Uninstalled shopping apps but reinstalls them when stressed.

→ Constraint: Shopping provides genuine emotional relief.

→ Emotional stake: “I know I'm doing this to myself, but it's one of the few things that makes me feel good.”

→ Desired transformation: Emotional trigger → alternative relief mechanism → spending becomes intentional rather than regulatory.

Example 4 — Family pressure

Broad topic: Financial Distress & Money Behavior

→ Submarket: Family financial pressure

→ Specific life situation: Relatives repeatedly request financial help.

→ Specific person: Adult supporting parents/younger siblings while managing own household.

→ Trigger: WhatsApp message asking for money.

Example 5 — Irregular income

Broad topic: Financial Distress & Money Behavior

→ Specific life situation: Income fluctuates dramatically month to month.

→ Specific person: Freelancer/commission earner/business owner.

→ Failed attempt: Uses the high month as the new spending baseline.

→ Constraint: Low-income months then force borrowing.

→ Emotional stake: “I can earn very well and still never feel secure.”

Example 6 — Financial reset

Broad topic: Financial Distress & Money Behavior

→ Specific life situation: Household income falls permanently.

→ Emotional stake: “I feel like I'm failing because I can no longer provide the life we used to have.”

That is much richer than “budget after job loss.”
