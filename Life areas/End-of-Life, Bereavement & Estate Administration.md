END-OF-LIFE, BEREAVEMENT & ESTATE ADMINISTRATION
The broad market

The deeper market isn't simply:

“People dealing with death.”

It is:

People suddenly navigating emotional, administrative, financial, relational and practical responsibilities before or after the death of someone important to them.

The crucial word is suddenly.

Someone can go from:

“I'm grieving my mother.”

to:

“I need to find her will.”

“Who has the house keys?”

“What accounts did she have?”

“What subscriptions are still charging?”

“Who needs to be notified?”

“What happens to her phone?”

“What does the funeral home need?”

“What did she want?”

“My siblings disagree about this.”

That transition from grief → responsibility is where a huge amount of the hidden market sits.

ONE CRITICAL DISTINCTION FOR THIS MARKET

I would not let our research accidentally turn this into a generic legal/estate-planning market.

There are three different layers:

Layer 1 — Organization

Things we can potentially help someone collect, track, organize and coordinate.

Examples:

document inventory
account inventory
task tracker
notification tracker
funeral checklist

Layer 2 — Emotional/practical transformation

Examples:

decision overload

belongings paralysis
family communication
caregiver resentment
first major milestone
return to normal life
Layer 3 — Professional/legal decisions

Examples:

probate
inheritance law
tax obligations
property transfer
debt liability
legal rights
jurisdiction-specific procedures

We should not pretend a generic product can replace lawyers, probate professionals, financial professionals, funeral professionals, or local authorities.

But that doesn't mean the market disappears.

Quite the opposite.

There is a huge layer between:

“I need a lawyer.”

and

“I have absolutely no idea what I need to organize before I speak to the lawyer.”

That coordination layer is where I see some of the most interesting opportunities.

That produces an enormous number of very specific moments:
