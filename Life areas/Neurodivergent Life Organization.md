AND I SEE 7 PARTICULARLY INTERESTING “HIDDEN MARKETS”

If I were taking this into the next research phase, I would put these at the top:

1. The Neurodivergent Life Rescue Market

People aren't looking for organization.

They're already behind and need to recover.

2. The Minimum-Viable-Life Market

People need systems designed for variable capacity, not ideal capacity.

3. The Neurodivergent Household Operating System

The problem is not one person. The household itself is failing.

4. The Transition Market

Moving, travel, new jobs, holidays, school changes, life changes.

5. The Relationship Follow-Through Market

Love exists. Intent exists. Execution fails.

That emotional contradiction is powerful:

“I care. Why can't my behavior show it?”

6. The Low-Friction Environment Market

Stop trying to make the person conform to the system.

Make the environment conform to how the person actually functions.

7. The System-Abandonment Market

Not:

“Here's another planner.”

But:

“Why do every organizational system I build eventually become another thing I have to maintain?”

That is a very different problem.

One final discipline I would add for this domain

For Postpartum, the life event itself often creates the market.

For Neurodivergent Life Organization, the market is often created by a repeated failure pattern.

So when we research each submarket, I would add one adversarial question:

“What repeatedly breaks despite the person already knowing what they are supposed to do?”

That question will prevent us from filling this domain with generic:

planners
habit trackers
to-do lists
productivity templates
ADHD tips
decluttering checklists
Notion dashboards.

Those are formats, not markets.

The real opportunities are things like:

life rescue → minimum viable functioning

variable capacity → capacity-aware planning

household chaos → behavior-compatible household system

relationship intention → reliable follow-through

transition disruption → transition-proof routine

information accumulation → trusted external memory

system abandonment → low-maintenance organization
