MARRIAGE, MONEY & COUPLES — MARKET ARCHITECTURE
1. The broad market
Marriage, Money & Couples

The underlying economic problem is not simply:

“Couples need to manage money.”

It is:

Two people with different histories, values, incomes, obligations, expectations and definitions of security must continuously make economic decisions together without damaging the relationship.

That creates a much larger opportunity surface.

I would map the market into at least 20 major submarkets, with several hidden markets underneath them.

A. NEWLY MARRIED / FINANCIAL MERGER

Your first submarket is correct, but it needs to go much deeper.

1. Newlywed Financial Integration

Life situations

just married
moving in together
first shared home
first joint account
first major purchase
first shared bills
moving from separate lifestyles to one household
discovering previously undisclosed financial obligations
Specific people
financially independent woman marrying financially independent man
higher-income spouse marrying lower-income spouse
saver marrying spender
financially sophisticated person marrying financially avoidant person
debt-free person marrying someone with significant debt
person accustomed to parental financial support marrying someone who expects independence
Triggers
first rent/mortgage
wedding expenses
honeymoon debt
first utility bills
first salary cycle
first major appliance/furniture purchase
discovering partner's debt
deciding whose account pays what
sending money to parents
Failed attempts

“We'll just split everything 50/50.”

“We'll figure it out as we go.”

“I'll handle the finances.”

“We don't need a budget; we're both responsible.”

Emotional stake

The argument isn't really about ₦50,000.

It becomes:

“You don't understand me.”
“You're irresponsible.”
“You don't trust me.”
“I'm carrying this marriage.”
“You think my money is yours.”
“You don't respect what I contribute.”
Desired transformation

Two separate financial lives → a mutually understood household financial system that both spouses trust.

BUT I WOULD ADD ONE MORE DIMENSION

This is important.

For the Postpartum research, we learned not to stop at “submarket.”

For Marriage & Money, I would add a relationship-state layer.

Because the same money problem has radically different economics depending on where the couple is.

Think:

Dating → engaged → newlywed → established marriage → first child → growing family → financial strain → financial shock → empty nest → retirement → separation → divorce → remarriage

Then cross that with:

income / debt / family / spending / autonomy / wealth / conflict / children / business / migration

That produces a much larger Customer Journey Graph rather than a flat niche list.

THE MOST IMPORTANT DISCOVERY

I think the biggest conceptual shift for this market is this:

Marriage + Money is not primarily a personal-finance market.

It is the intersection of:

Money × Relationship × Power × Fairness × Trust × Identity × Family × Future

And that's why generic products such as:

couple budget planner
marriage financial workbook
money conversation cards
couples finance spreadsheet

are generally sitting at the surface layer.

The deeper opportunities are things like:

“We keep fighting about helping our parents.”

“My husband earns less than me and money has changed how we relate.”

“I'm tired of being the household CFO.”

“My wife won't look at the finances.”

“We don't agree on what a financially secure life looks like.”

“His family keeps asking us for money.”

“We discovered a secret debt and don't know how to rebuild trust.”

“We make good money but somehow never get ahead.”

“We are starting over financially after moving countries.”

“We remarried but our money still belongs to two separate families.”

Those are markets, because they contain a person, situation, trigger, failed solution, emotional consequence and desired transformation.
