1. Dinner decision fatigue - underlying problem: the daily dinner decision is unbounded and lands on one person
2. Picky eaters - underlying problem: a childs food range is narrow and mealtimes became a battleground
3. Food chaining - this is an intervention, not a market; underlying problem: a child eats almost no foods and needs a structured way to widen that without fights
4. Lunchboxes - underlying problem: a packed lunch must be made under time pressure AND actually eaten

7. Grocery organization - underlying problem: the weekly shop is where my time and money leak

# SUBMARKETS (expanded, drilled - research-grounded)

## 1. Dinner Decision Fatigue

Core evidence: 85pct of parents think about dinner even when not cooking (E1). Meal planners make 4 decisions/day, 28/week, 1,400+/year (E2). 46pct would delete social media to never plan dinner again (E3). 8 in 10 households rely on one parent for meals (E1). 30pct say disagreements arise daily over what to eat or who cooks (E1). 69pct say it is easier to handle meal planning themselves than to hand it off (E1). 115 min average total dinner operation per day (E1). 45pct turn to processed/prepackaged foods when planning becomes too much (E6). 42pct of Australians report ordering takeaway at least once a week simply because they cannot decide what to eat (E6).

### Micro-situation A - The 5:15pm arrival with no plan
- **Situation:** 5:15pm on a weekday; two primary-school children are hungry and asking; the parent has just walked in; there is no dinner plan and the fridge is partly empty.
- **Person/context:** Default household food-manager mother of two primary-school children (ages 6 and 8), full-time salaried job, home about 5:30pm, 30-45 min before homework/bath/bedtime start; partner usually not home until 7. [HYPOTHESIS: archetypal details constructed, not survey-derived]
- **Trigger:** She arrives and realizes the planned meal cannot happen today (forgot to thaw the protein / a child has an activity / she is too tired to cook the planned recipe).
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** She planned meals on Sunday, but Wednesday schedule changed, the planned recipe needed chicken she forgot to thaw, kids rejected the substitute, partner will not be home until 8 - so she ordered delivery. The Sunday plan repeatedly collapses when real life breaks it; just decide in the moment fails because she is depleted. [RESEARCH EVIDENCE: 49pct of meal planners wonder what to make 2-3x/week; top reasons: too tired, did not plan, nothing sounds appealing (E2); 77pct of parents say May/June most stressful for meal prep (E19); 80pct say everyday tasks get in the way of preparing meals (E19)]
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** She feels trapped in being solely responsible for producing dinner and resentful that the family treats it as something she should magically produce; it reads to her as proof she is failing at the manager role she never chose. [RESEARCH EVIDENCE: 67pct feel stuck being the food manager (E2); 72pct feel overwhelmed by responsibilities (E2); 75pct wish someone else would handle meal decisions (E2)]
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: When the original plan breaks, dinner automatically reconfigures around what is actually available, who is home, and how much energy she has - not another 5pm interrogation.

Drill-down (D6): The failure cascade (Sunday plan -> Wed change -> forgotten thaw -> rejected substitute -> partner late -> delivery) suggests the product is not a meal planner but a dinner decision engine that dynamically replans the remaining week when reality breaks the plan, factoring thaw-time, who is home, and energy. [MODEL INFERENCE] [RESEARCH EVIDENCE: 40pct of parents think AI could do a better job planning dinner start-to-finish (E5); 67pct of non-parents believe they could outperform AI but parents are more open to handing it off (E5)]

### Micro-situation B - The group-chat that never decides (situation, not submarket)
- **Situation:** A family group text asks whats for dinner daily; answers arrive at different times; no one owns the decision; default becomes takeout.
- **Person/context:** Two full-time parents with a shared phone; kids old enough to text; neither wants to be the decider. [HYPOTHESIS]
- **Trigger:** About 4:30pm; everyone replies piecemeal; no owner.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried whoever gets home first decides - that parent is then resentful; tried a shared note - ignored by day 4.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** Low-grade marital friction; both feel basic adulthood should not be this hard and quietly blame each other for the vacuum. [RESEARCH EVIDENCE: 25pct of couples get in food fights about dinner 1-2x/week (E3); 44pct of couples struggle to come up with a dinner plan most nights (E3); 53pct say not having to plan and cook dinner is the hottest form of foreplay (E3); 55pct of parents admitted they would get intimate more often if their partner handled dinner (E3)]
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A low-effort shared default mechanism (rotation/owned days) that kills the daily text without either parent carrying it alone.

### Micro-situation C - The two-dinner household (partner food conflict, situation)
- **Situation:** One parent wants a home-cooked/healthy dinner, the other wants fast/takeout; they relitigate it nightly.
- **Person/context:** Couple, different food values + energy; the healthy-eater is also often the cook. [HYPOTHESIS]
- **Trigger:** 5pm; escalates on hard days.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried compromise meals - pleased no one; tried separate dinners - double work + resentment.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** The healthy-eater feels their values (and labor) are unsupported; the takeout-eater feels policed. Each reads it as you do not care about my needs.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: One dinner satisfying both values without two cooks or a nightly negotiation.

### Micro-situation D - Extended-family drop-in (cultural secondary situation)
- **Situation:** Relatives arrive hungry/unannounced; food is expected; the parent must produce a meal beyond the plan.
- **Person/context:** Diaspora/extended-family household where drop-ins are normal; the mother is host-cook. [HYPOTHESIS - cultural secondary lens]
- **Trigger:** Weekend visit; no notice.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** She tried keep extra staples but they go stale; tried tell them not to come - family friction.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** Hospitality is part of her identity/pride, but the unplanned labor makes her feel her household is never hers; saying no feels like failing her culture.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A guest-ready buffer (freezer/staples) that absorbs drop-ins without daily extra cooking. [MODEL INFERENCE] (secondary lens)

## 2. Picky Eaters

Core evidence: 80pct of parents cooking for picky eaters (E5). 71pct of parents describe their kids as picky (E4). 58pct find it difficult to find foods the whole family enjoys (E4). Parents modify meals 56pct of the time to accommodate pickiness (E4). 25pct of adults skip preparing specific foods because they are not confident using a knife (E5). 15pct of ages 30-44 cite picky eaters as third most popular obstacle to home cooking (E20).

### Micro-situation A - The toddler on 5 foods
- **Situation:** A 3-year-old eats only nuggets, pasta, bread, apple, yoghurt; mealtimes are battles; daycare notes he barely eats.
- **Person/context:** First-time parents [HYPOTHESIS: both anxious], one default-cook; child in high-chair stage.
- **Trigger:** Mealtime; a rejected new food; daycare comment.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried bribing (one bite of broccoli = dessert) - he ate it once then refused; tried a separate safe meal - now he expects it; pediatric leaflet said generic keep offering.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They fear he is malnourished and that failing to feed him properly marks them as inadequate parents; mealtimes have become the part of the day they dread most.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A low-pressure system that widens accepted foods at his pace, without turning dinner into a negotiation.

### Micro-situation B - The beige only school-age eater
- **Situation:** A 7-year-old refuses anything not beige/recognizable; birthday parties and school expose it.
- **Person/context:** Parents embarrassed at others houses and school; child now socially aware.
- **Trigger:** Parties, school lunches, relatives houses.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried hidden-veg blends - he detected them, trust broke; tried you do not leave the table till you try - meltdown, no eating.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They feel judged by other parents as having failed and worry their child will be the one everyone notices will not eat. [RESEARCH EVIDENCE: 60pct of parents say their children refuse to eat a meal served at least once a week (E17); families estimate GBP283/yr wasted on uneaten kid food (E17)]
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: Child widens range at his own pace; parents stop covert tricks that erode trust.

### Micro-situation C - The teenage picky eater (different problem)
- **Situation:** A 15-year-old controls his own food, rejects family meals, eats instant noodles alone.
- **Person/context:** Parents of a teen who is home before them after school.
- **Trigger:** Teen autonomy; family dinners decline to none.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried sit at the table or no food - he ate nothing / bought his own; tried cooking separately - he still will not eat their food.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They fear he will never build real eating habits and that the family table - the thing that held them together - is gone, and it is their fault. [RESEARCH EVIDENCE: only 21pct of parents with children aged 5-17 say their kids are often involved in cooking (E11); just 60pct of 13-17 year olds can cook three or more meals (E11)]
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: Teen builds a few real meals he owns; one or two family dinners restored without control.

### Micro-situation D - The neurodivergent selective eater (ARFID-adjacent) [MODEL INFERENCE]
- **Situation:** An autistic child eats a tiny set by texture/colour; new foods cause distress; mealtimes are traumatic.
- **Person/context:** Parents of a neurodivergent child; OT waitlist long.
- **Trigger:** Any attempt to add food leads to meltdown.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried just try a bite - distress escalates; tried ignoring it - no progress; waitlisted for feeding therapy months out.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They fear for his health and grieve that something as basic as eating is a source of pain for him; they feel isolated from meal-inviting social life.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A sensory-safe, ordered expansion path (see section 3) done without trauma. **Safety:** overlaps ARFID - signpost OT/clinical support, not a cure.

## 3. Selective-Eating Expansion (Food Chaining reframed - intervention, not a market)

High-scrutiny market - kept SEPARATE from section 2 Picky Eaters: different buyers, urgency, safety constraints, willingness to pay, professional involvement, and acceptable interventions. Not a severity extension.

### Micro-situation A - The parent who found food chaining but cannot execute it
- **Situation:** A parent of a severely selective eater finds the food-chaining concept but cannot run it alone for THEIR child.
- **Person/context:** Parent of a child (often neurodivergent) eating fewer than 10 foods; has internet access, no current OT.
- **Trigger:** OT mention / online discovery; realizes random exposure does not work.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried a generic chaining book - steps do not start from their childs exact safe foods; tried add one new food weekly - child rejects, no bridge.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They feel hope then overwhelm, and fear doing it wrong will set the child back further.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A personalized chain built from the childs exact safe foods to adjacent foods, in the correct order.

### Micro-situation B - The therapy-gap home bridge
- **Situation:** A family in (or waiting for) feeding therapy wants to continue chaining at home between sessions.
- **Person/context:** Family with a feeding plan but gaps between appointments.
- **Trigger:** Therapy gap; regression risk during school holidays.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried remembering the exact next step - cannot; therapist unreachable between visits.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They fear losing hard-won progress and letting the child down during the gap.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A home bridge that continues the chain between sessions. **Safety:** technique, not diagnosis; flag OT support for ARFID-level restriction.

## 4. Lunchboxes

Core evidence: Parents spend 2 hrs/week packing school lunches (E4). 32pct of packed lunches are uneaten (E4). 34pct say finding foods kids will eat is biggest lunchbox hurdle (E4); 34pct say keeping lunches interesting is a hurdle (E4); 30pct cite food waste as a hurdle (E4). 47pct of parents regularly plan their childrens school lunches; nearly 1 in 5 who do not say they simply lack the energy (E6). Those who plan save over 50 dollars/week vs canteen = 2,500+ dollars/year (E6).

### Micro-situation A - The morning scramble
- **Situation:** Parent assembles kids lunchboxes daily under time pressure and hates it.
- **Person/context:** A salaried parent with a hard 7:45 departure for an 8:30 school run; two school-age children; no time to repack lunches at home.
- **Trigger:** School mornings; already rushing; child remembers an item too late.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried pack the night before - forgotten in the fridge rush; tried letting kids pack - unbalanced/uneaten.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They feel the morning is a small daily failure and guilt if the lunch comes home uneaten.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A prep-ahead system that takes 5 min, not 25, and the kids will eat.

### Micro-situation B - The comes home uneaten lunch
- **Situation:** Child returns lunchbox full; parent doubts the child ate.
- **Person/context:** A working parent whose 7-year-old eats reliably at home but routinely returns from school with lunch untouched.
- **Trigger:** Daily; notes from school.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried packing healthy the child will not eat; tried peer-cool snacks - still partly uneaten.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They feel helpless about their childs nutrition during the 6 hours they cannot see, and that their effort is invisible/wasted. [RESEARCH EVIDENCE: 32pct of packed lunches uneaten on typical day (E4)]
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A lunch the child actually eats within the parents rules.

### Micro-situation C - The teen lunchbox (or not)
- **Situation:** A 14-year-old is too old for a cute box but needs food; rejects what parent packs.
- **Person/context:** Parent of teen; school with canteen.
- **Trigger:** Teen embarrassment; canteen-money battles.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried packed lunch - mocked; gave cash - spent on snacks.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They fear the teen eats junk and that handing him money is them giving up on caring.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A teen-owned lunch plan (or canteen budget) that works for both.

### Micro-situation D - The cultural-lunchbox navigation (secondary lens) [MODEL INFERENCE]
- **Situation:** A child from a Nigerian/African/immigrant home brings culturally specific food and is scrutinized, or wants normal lunch.
- **Person/context:** Immigrant parent; child negotiating identity at school.
- **Trigger:** School; peer comments; child asks for sandwiches.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried sending heritage food - child embarrassed; tried sandwiches - feels like erasing home.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** The parent wants to pass on heritage and belonging at once, and reads the lunchbox as a small daily referendum on who the child is allowed to be.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: Lunches that honor heritage AND feel acceptable to the child.

## 5. Freezer Usability (Freezer Systems reframed - one label, 4+ buyers)

Core evidence: 52pct of adults are NOT meal preppers (E5). 38pct do not have groceries on hand when needed (E5). Households that freeze food more are 40pct more likely to report less waste (E7). 56pct of households report wasting less after grocery price pressure - freezing is one of the top adaptation behaviors (E7). 43pct discard food near/past date label (E15); relying on date labels = 8.9 cups waste/week vs 4.0 cups for those who do not (E15). 76pct of Americans admit to having tossed out food items (E5); 42pct let items expire; 31pct have an aversion to leftovers (E5).

### Micro-situation A - Batch-cooks but cannot find the food Tuesday
- **Situation:** Family batch-cooks Sunday but food gets lost/burned/unlabeled; by Tuesday it is unusable.
- **Person/context:** Batch-cooking parent; busy household; chest freezer.
- **Trigger:** Intention to save time backfires; mystery containers.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried labels once - pen smeared; tried I will remember - did not.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They feel the system they built to help is itself proof they cannot stay organized.
- **Desired Transformation:** From food stored but unusable -> to reliably knowing what is available, where it is, and what should be used next. [RESEARCH EVIDENCE: households that increased freezing behavior were among those most likely to report waste reduction (E7)]

### Micro-situation B - Wants an emergency-meal buffer for unpredictable days
- **Situation:** Parent wants ready meals for bad/no-shop weeks but never builds the buffer.
- **Person/context:** Working parent; unpredictable week.
- **Trigger:** A week with no time to cook; illness.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried cook extra and freeze - eaten same week; never reaches buffer size.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They fear relying on takeout means admitting they cannot run their own home.
- **Desired Transformation:** From no safety net on bad/no-shop weeks -> to a reliable buffer of ready meals that absorbs unpredictable days.

### Micro-situation C - Cooks for a dietary-restricted family member, needs separate inventory
- **Situation:** One member has allergies/condition; their freezer items mix with everyones and get eaten.
- **Person/context:** Parent managing a childs allergy or a condition diet (cross-link M04).
- **Trigger:** A cross-contamination scare; the restricted item vanished.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried a separate shelf - ignored; tried color labels - not understood by others.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They fear a mistake will harm the family member and that no one else takes the constraint seriously.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A separate, legible inventory the household respects.

### Micro-situation D - Full freezer, no confidence what is usable
- **Situation:** Freezer is full but the parent does not trust dates/safety of items.
- **Person/context:** Household with bulk-shop habits; no dating system.
- **Trigger:** Defrosting something questionable; guilt throwing out.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried use it or lose it - gambled and wasted; tried a list - not updated.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They feel money leaking invisibly and anxiety about feeding the family from food they cannot trust.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A trusted dating/visibility system so nothing is guesswork.

## 6. Family Meal Rotation

Core evidence: 86pct of adults are meal repeaters (E5). 60pct repeat because it ensures everyone eats what they like (E5). 21pct repeat because they lack energy for new recipes (E5). 58pct say recipe boredom will make them cook less next year (E5). 52pct did not try a new food/ingredient last month (E5). 26pct cook certain meals on specific days of the week (E5).

### Micro-situation A - The 4-meal loop boredom
- **Situation:** Family eats the same 4 meals; everyone is tired of them but no one decides new ones.
- **Person/context:** Default-cook parent; two kids; partner indifferent.
- **Trigger:** Repetition; complaints.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried find new recipes - need shopping they did not plan; tried a recipe app - too many choices, paralysis.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They resent that even the small act of choosing variety falls on them alone.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A rotating repertoire (12-16 meals) on autopilot.

### Micro-situation B - Rotation that satisfies mixed needs (picky + healthy + quick)
- **Situation:** Parent wants a rotation covering a picky kid + healthy adult + quick cook.
- **Person/context:** A household where one child is a selective eater, the adult wants quick healthy dinners, and the cook has about 40 min between work and the kids activities.
- **Trigger:** Planning Sunday; conflicting constraints.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried one meal for all - someone loses; tried separate - exhaustion.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They feel no one is fully served and that compromise means everyone mildly unhappy.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A rotation with built-in flexible swaps per person.

### Micro-situation C - Weekday-weekend collapse (recurring failure state)
- **Situation:** Structured weekdays; weekends (no routine, activities, tired) lead to takeout/junk.
- **Person/context:** A family whose Saturdays hold a 9am football match and a 2pm birthday party, leaving no meal routine.
- **Trigger:** Friday night; sports; late wakes.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried planning all weekend meals - too ambitious, collapsed; tried keep weekday system running - broke on leisure.
- **Emotional Stake (meaning):** They feel the weekday effort is undone and that weekends reveal they never had a real system.
- **Desired Transformation:** Before -> After: A loose weekend rhythm needing no willpower.

### Micro-situation D - Holiday / special-occasion overwhelm (event situation)
- **Situation:** Parent handles a large occasion meal and is overwhelmed by scale.
- **Person/context:** Holiday host.
- **Trigger:** Recurring holiday; guests.
- **Failed Attempt/Constraint:** They tried do it all Thursday - exhausted by event; tried delegating - others under-deliver.

## 13. Screen-Time at the Table
