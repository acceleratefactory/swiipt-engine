IN-LAWS, BOUNDARIES & FAMILY RELATIONSHIPS
Broad Market

At the highest level, this market is about:

Managing relationships with extended family while protecting the couple's autonomy, household, parenting decisions, emotional safety, and sense of belonging.

There are several forces constantly colliding:

Marriage/partnership × parents × siblings × children × culture × money × loyalty × expectations × proximity × obligation × identity

And that creates a much larger market than simply “difficult in-laws.”

01 — MARRIAGE VS FAMILY LOYALTY

This is one of the deepest territories.

The “Whose Side Are You On?” Market

Specific situations:

“I don't need you to hate your family. I need to know that we're a team.”

That's substantially deeper than “in-law conflict.”

The most important distinction

I would be very careful not to turn this into a generic:

“How to set boundaries with toxic family”

market.

That's too broad and increasingly cliché.

The richer opportunity is to identify the specific boundary being violated, the relationship between the people, the life stage, the trigger, and the cost of enforcing the boundary.

For example:

“Mother-in-law conflict”

is broad.

But:

“New mother whose mother-in-law repeatedly overrides feeding and sleep decisions, while her husband refuses to confront his mother because she provides childcare.”

is a specific market.

And:

“Married professional whose parents expect monthly financial support after she relocates abroad, while her husband believes their obligations should now prioritize their children's future.”

is another.

And:

“Stepfather who is expected to provide financially for children but is not allowed to discipline them.”

is another.

And:

“Couple living with an aging parent who financially helped them buy the house and now expects veto power over household decisions.”

is another.

Those are the kinds of hidden markets I would want the research engine to surface.

The final research should therefore not merely tell us “in-law conflict is a big market.” It should reveal hundreds of specific relationship situations, then let us identify which ones have the strongest combination of frequency + intensity + emotional stake + failed existing solutions + willingness to pay + repeatability + transformation potential.

That is the same Postpartum discipline, but applied correctly to the relationship system rather than stopping at generic family-conflict topics.
