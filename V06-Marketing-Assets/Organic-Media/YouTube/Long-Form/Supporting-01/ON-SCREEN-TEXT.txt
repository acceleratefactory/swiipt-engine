ON-SCREEN TEXT (deterministic; short lines only)

[The offer everyone makes]
  'Wake me if you need help.' It is well-meant, it is common, and it fails almost every time.
  The reason is not the intention.
[It transfers the decision, not the work]
  Think about what 'wake me if you need help' actually asks.
  It asks the person who is already awake and already exhausted to judge, in the moment, whether the situation counts as needing help.
[The 3 AM calculation]
  So the tired brain runs a calculation it has run every night: is this bad enough to wake him?
  He has work.
[What replaces it]
  Replace the offer with a default.
  Not 'wake me if you need me', but 'this block is mine'.
[The hand-off that does not need you to ask]
  The other half is the hand-off.
  If your partner genuinely sleeps deeply, an offer to wake is close to useless - so the system stops depending on hearing.
[Keeping the goodwill, losing the guesswork]
  None of this means the original offer was bad.
  It means goodwill is not a system.
[The two objections you will hear]
  When you replace the offer with a block, you will probably hear two things.
  The first is 'I don't hear the baby.' That is not an excuse; it is a description of deep sleep, and it is exactly why the system uses an alarm and a fixed handover time instead of relying on a cry.
[Why this is not about fairness points]
  One caution.
  The point of a named block is not to keep score of who is more tired, or to prove that one of you does more.
[Start tonight]
  Tonight, replace one offer with one block.
  Write one name and two clock times.
