NARRATION (verbatim - no AI voice assumed, no invented claims)
Deliver calmly, at a normal pace. No hype, no urgency, no advertisement tone.

'Wake me if you need help.' It is well-meant, it is common, and it fails almost every time. The reason is not the intention. The reason is what the sentence actually transfers.

Think about what 'wake me if you need help' actually asks. It asks the person who is already awake and already exhausted to judge, in the moment, whether the situation counts as needing help. That is not a transfer of work. It is a transfer of a decision - to the person least equipped to make it. And the person least likely to make it at all, because asking for help at 3 AM feels like a bigger deal than just doing one more nappy.

So the tired brain runs a calculation it has run every night: is this bad enough to wake him? He has work. It is only twenty minutes in. I am awake anyway. And the answer is almost always no. Not because the help would be refused, but because the bar for asking is set by exhaustion - and exhaustion always sets it too high.

Replace the offer with a default. Not 'wake me if you need me', but 'this block is mine'. A named shift with fixed clock times. The decision is made once, in daylight, by both adults, and then it is never made again at 3 AM. The schedule asks - not you.

The other half is the hand-off. If your partner genuinely sleeps deeply, an offer to wake is close to useless - so the system stops depending on hearing. The shift has an alarm, and the baby is handed over at a fixed time. That is the difference between a plan that survives contact with a real night and one that does not.

None of this means the original offer was bad. It means goodwill is not a system. Keep the intention. Change the mechanism. The person who offered still carries the shift - it is just written down, so nobody has to decide in the dark whether to ask for it.

When you replace the offer with a block, you will probably hear two things. The first is 'I don't hear the baby.' That is not an excuse; it is a description of deep sleep, and it is exactly why the system uses an alarm and a fixed handover time instead of relying on a cry. The second is 'just wake me.' Answer it once, in daylight: waking you is itself a decision I have to make while exhausted, and that decision is what keeps failing. A fixed block takes the decision away from both of us.

One caution. The point of a named block is not to keep score of who is more tired, or to prove that one of you does more. The moment it becomes a scoreboard, the plan turns into another argument. The block exists so that rest becomes predictable rather than negotiated. Predictable rest is what actually reduces the resentment - not winning the comparison.

Tonight, replace one offer with one block. Write one name and two clock times. That is the whole change - and it is the change that makes the rest of the system possible.
