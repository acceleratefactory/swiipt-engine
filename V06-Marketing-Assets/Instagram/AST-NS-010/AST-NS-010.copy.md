## Visual copy (on image, verbatim from paired design spec)

> The word your partner hears every night: 'helper.'

> It creates a hierarchy. One parent owns the night. The other just shows up when called.

> The alternative: co-manager. Named shifts. A schedule that asks — not begs.

> When you change the word, you change the ownership.

> Read the night-shift guide

## Post copy (verbatim from asset record)

**Story copy (verbatim):**

1. (hook) A request creates a favour. A schedule creates a default. Giving your partner a named shift is a status upgrade, not charity.
2. (problem) Asking directly for help can feel like an accusation. The partner's one-off gesture resets the expectation: he helped once, so the default remains she owns all of it.
3. (insight) Reframes the partner from 'helper' to 'co-manager' — a status upgrade instead of a request for charity. A written roster removes the emotional labour of asking: the schedule asks, not her.
4. (cta) Nights with zero protected block become a protected block of 3+ hours on most nights. 'Whose turn' arguments drop from several times a week to once a week or less. The partner initiates his shift without being reminded.
