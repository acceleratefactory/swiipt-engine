Most couples try to fix nights with effort. The effort is real - it just cannot solve a design problem.

A written night contract does three things a conversation cannot:

1. It removes the emotional labour of asking. The schedule asks, not you.
2. It gives your partner a named shift rather than vague "help" - a status upgrade, not charity.
3. It makes your protected sleep block real by scheduling the milk hand-off in advance, so it does not depend on a 3 AM negotiation.

If you are breastfeeding, the honest objection is that "sleep through the night" is not simply available to you. So the system does not promise that. It schedules the hand-off instead: your partner brings the baby to you, you feed, your partner takes the baby back to the crib - and your block becomes real even though you are the one feeding.

There is also a simple ledger - the sleep banker - so that invisible exhaustion becomes visible and tradeable: one credit for covering a three-to-five-hour night block, one payback as a morning sleep-in or a three-hour afternoon nap, redeemed within seven days.
