[There is no single correct roster]
  The first thing to accept is that there is no single correct way to split a newborn night.
  There are three workable models, and the right one depends on who is actually available at night, not on what anyone thinks a fair split should look like on paper.
[Template A - the breastfeeding-compatible split]
  Template A is for a household where a partner is present and willing, and the mother is breastfeeding.
  The night is cut into named blocks.
[Template B - alternating nights]
  Template B is for formula or expressed feeding, where either adult can run a whole feed.
  You split the night into two long windows, and you alternate which adult owns which window on odd and even nights.
[Roster C - no partner available]
  Roster C is for the household where a partner works away, is often absent at night, or is simply not in the home.
  It does not pretend that a two-adult split is available.
[How to choose in two minutes]
  The routing question is one line: who is available at night?
  If a partner is present and willing, you are in A or B.
[What each model asks of each adult]
  Be honest about the cost of each.
  Template A asks the partner to stay awake and alert for a defined block - that is real work, and it is why the block is shorter than a full night.
[The mistake almost everyone makes first]
  The most common mistake is choosing the fairest-looking model instead of the one that fits the household.
  A fifty-fifty split that requires a deep-sleeping parent to wake on a cry will fail within a week.
[Fill in the clock times]
  Whichever model you pick, it only exists once the clock times are written.
  Two times, one name per block, on paper.
