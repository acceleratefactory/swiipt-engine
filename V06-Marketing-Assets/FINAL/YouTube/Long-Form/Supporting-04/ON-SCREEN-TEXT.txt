ON-SCREEN TEXT (deterministic; short lines only)

[The 3 AM argument is really an 8 PM gap]
  The 3 AM argument almost never happens because of the 3 AM situation.
  It happens because a decision that should have been made at 8 PM was never made at all.
[What the fridge chart is]
  The fridge chart is deliberately unglamorous.
  It is two clock times and one name per block, written by hand, in the same place every day.
[Why it lives on the fridge]
  An app can hold more information than a sheet of paper.
  But information you have to open is information you will renegotiate.
[Filling it in - a worked example]
  The way it holds a real household: the working parent names the block they can actually keep, usually one before their own sleep or one after it.
  The other parent names the block around the feed hand-offs.
[The 8 PM five-minute review]
  Before the night starts, you spend five minutes on it.
  Who is on tonight, do the times still make sense, and is there anything unusual tomorrow - an early start, a trip, a hard day?
[Where the chart breaks and why]
  Most charts break in one of three places.
  They become too detailed, so nobody fills them in.
[The first week is the hardest]
  Do not judge the chart on night one.
  The first nights feel strange precisely because the decision is no longer yours to make in the moment - and if you are the parent who has been carrying every night, handing over can feel harder than staying up.
[When the chart changes]
  The chart is not a contract carved in stone; it is a decision that gets re-decided weekly, because the baby's sleep pattern changes and so does your work.
  The rule is that renegotiating is expected and scheduled - never improvised at 3 AM.
