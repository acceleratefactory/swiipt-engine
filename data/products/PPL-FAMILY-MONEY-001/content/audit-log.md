# 30-Day Use Audit Log + Resell
*The honest check on what you actually used. Run it after thirty days, then turn the unused items back into fund money.*

## Why a dated audit
Buying is a guess; use is a fact. The audit replaces "I think we needed it" with "we used it this many times", read from your own log. It is not a judgement on the past month. It is the information the next month's plan was missing.

## The log
Add one row when something is bought. Fill the use count as the weeks pass. Do not try to remember at the end — that is what the log is for.

## The day-30 review (20 minutes)
1. Read each row. Count the uses honestly.
2. Mark the verdict:
   - **Used well** — it earned its place. Keep it, and keep buying this kind of thing.
   - **Used a little** — keep it only if the small use was essential. Otherwise treat it as a Wait next time.
   - **Never used** — it was a fear-buy. It goes to resell, not to the back of a cupboard.
3. For every "never used", list it for resell today, at the price you can realistically get. Any amount returned is fund money.
4. Add the resell amount to the baby fund on the One-Number Budget Chart.

> ## The rule that makes this work
> An unused item is not a loss you carry. It is fund money sitting in the wrong form. Resell it and it returns to the plan.

**What to log, and what not to.** Log the things you were unsure about — the Wait items that became Buys, the gear, the extras. You do not need to log feeding and nappies every day; those are recurring and already sit in the monthly figure.

- Fed by: the Buy / Wait / Never List.

- Refreshed by: the Corner-Shop Cost-Swap Card when a cost keeps overspending.
- This log is also the record the monthly check-in reads, so the plan is measured against real use rather than memory.

## Scope note
This is a self-reported household log. It is general organisation, not regulated financial advice, debt counselling or investment advice. Resell prices are set by your own market, and you decide the price you will accept.
