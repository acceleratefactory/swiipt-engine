# Corner-Shop Cost-Swap Card
*When a weekly cost keeps overspending, swap it once instead of re-deciding it every week. Three swaps, chosen from your own market, not from a list of national averages.*

## How a swap works
1. Find the one cost that overspends most weeks. Not the biggest cost — the one that goes over.
2. Write what you buy now, and the lower-cost equivalent you can actually reach (the corner shop, the market, the bulk pack, the refill).
3. Swap it once. Check the difference at the end of the week.
4. If it holds, keep it. If it fails, the swap was wrong, not you.

**Filling the card honestly.**
- **Lower-cost equivalent**, not "cheaper and worse". If the substitute makes the week harder, it is not a swap.
- **Where** must be somewhere you already pass. A saving you must travel for is usually not a saving.
- **Difference per week** — write what you measured, not what you hope. If you have not measured it yet, leave it blank until the week ends.
- **Checked on** — every swap carries the date you priced it. Prices move; a swap confirmed in one month may be wrong in three.

> ## The rule of three
> Change three costs. Not ten. Three swaps you actually keep beat ten you abandon by the second week.

## Run it in three steps
1. **Tonight (5 minutes):** fill the first row only — the cost that overspends most.
2. **This week:** make that one swap and note the difference when the week closes.
3. **Next week:** if it held, add a second swap. Stop at three until all three are steady.

**When a swap is not the answer.** If the cost is already the lowest you can reach and still overspends, the problem is not the shop — it is the figure. Take it back to the One-Number Budget Chart and the audit rather than forcing a worse swap.

## Scope note
Swaps are your own cost decisions in your own market. This is general household organisation, not regulated financial advice or debt counselling, and it does not promise savings.
