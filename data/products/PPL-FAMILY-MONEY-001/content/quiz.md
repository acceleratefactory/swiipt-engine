# The Real Baby Budget Quiz
*Do this once, tonight, with the account you actually pay from. It ends with one number: your household's first-year baby figure. Around 15 minutes for the first pass.*

## Why this is a quiz and not an average
No universal baby number exists. A blended average built elsewhere tells you nothing about your market, your tier, your week or your baby. This quiz does not give you an average to live up to. It converts the spending you already know about into one figure you and your partner can both see, and it says where the gaps are.

If you cannot finish because a figure is genuinely unknown, mark it `?` and keep going. A number with three honest gaps beats a tidy number you invented.

## Step 1 — List the recurring costs (5 minutes)
Write what leaves the household every month for the baby. Use your own market and your own words. Leave a row blank rather than guess.

| Cost | What it covers | Amount per month | How often it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feeding | Formula, or your own expressed-milk supplies | | Weekly prices move |
| Nappies and wipes | Day and night use | | Pack size changes the price |
| Washing and drying | Extra loads the baby creates | | Seasonal |
| Health basics | The ordinary items kept in the house | | Occasional |
| Transport | The extra trips the baby adds | | Fuel and fares move |
| Childcare or help | Any paid help, however irregular | | Agreement-based |
| `?` | Anything you pay for but have not named | | |

## Step 3 — Turn the two into one monthly figure (3 minutes)
1. Add the monthly column from Step 1.
2. Take the yearly total from Step 2 and divide it by twelve.
3. Add the two. That is your baby figure for a normal month.
4. Now write the range, because your income is not always the same.

| Line | Your figure |
|---|---|
| Monthly recurring (Step 1) | |
| Yearly items spread across twelve months (Step 2 ÷ 12) | |
| **Normal-month baby figure** | |
| Lean-month figure (what you can still cover when income dips) | |
| Peak-month figure (when several yearly items land together) | |

> ## One number, one household
> The number is not a target to earn. It is a picture you both share, so a purchase is judged against a plan instead of decided in fear.

## Step 4 — Check it against reality, once (3 minutes)
Look at the last two months of actual spending on the baby and compare.

- If your real spending is far above the figure: the gap is the discovery, not a failure. Add the missing row to Step 1 and read the figure again.
- If it is far below: keep the figure as it is. The lower months are not the plan; the plan is what a normal month costs.
- If your income is irregular: the range (lean / normal / peak) is the honest answer. Use all three on the chart, not one.

## What this quiz is not
It is general household organisation, not regulated financial advice, debt counselling or investment advice. It never promises savings. It cannot tell you what you can afford; it shows you what you are already spending and what the plan needs to cover.

## Dated inputs
Any figure you write carries the date you checked it. Prices for feeding, nappies and transport move with the market, the week and the pack size. The one-number method stays the same; the numbers are refreshed, not the system.

## Example (illustrative only — not your figures, not a recommended amount)
A household that pays monthly for feeding, nappies, washing and transport, and yearly for larger gear and clothing, might reach a normal-month figure in the low five figures of its own currency. Your figure will differ, and it should. The example shows the shape of the arithmetic, nothing more.

Date this example was written: September 2026. Re-check prices in your own market before using any number.
