# The Argument That Ends the War
*Why "just ask for help" keeps failing - and what replaces it.*

## What is actually happening

You are not failing at sleep. You are running an unowned shift. A newborn wakes every night, but nobody agreed in advance whose turn it is - so every night is renegotiated by two exhausted adults, and the default quietly becomes: you own all of it.

That is why "can you help more?" produces a one-off gesture (one Saturday morning) instead of a system. A request creates a favour. A schedule creates a default.

## The one thing that changes it

> **The night does not need more goodwill. It needs an owner and a roster.**

A written night contract does three things a conversation cannot:

1. Removes the emotional labour of asking - the schedule asks, not you.
2. Gives your partner a named shift, not "help" - a status upgrade, not charity.
3. Makes your protected sleep block real by scheduling the milk hand-off in advance.

## Why the breastfeeding objection is real - and solvable

If you are breastfeeding, "sleep through the night" is not simply available to you. The contract does not pretend otherwise. It schedules a hand-off (a pumped bottle, or your partner brings the baby to you to feed and returns her to the crib) so that your block is real, not fictional.

## What this is not

This is an operating system for how two adults divide the night. It is not baby sleep training, and it is not a substitute for medical or mental-health care. When exhaustion is more than tiredness, stop and route out (see the rescue card).

Before you run any roster, both adults read and agree the **safety guardrails** in Tonight's Shift Chart - infant safe sleep, staying fully awake on shift, no earplugs or sleep aids on shift, and the rule that the baby always overrides the roster.

**Educational content - not medical, clinical, or mental-health advice.**
