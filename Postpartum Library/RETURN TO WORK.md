**Key demand signals found (cite these):**
1. **Nigeria leave is short & breastfeeding at work is unsupported.** Alive & Thrive *Maternity Entitlements in Nigeria*: statutory 12 wks @50% pay (federal 16 wks @100%); Lagos/Enugu/Kaduna 24 wks. But **only 5% of Nigerian employers provide on-site childcare** and there is **no legal mandate for a lactation room** (IFC *Investing in Childcare in Nigeria*, 2023). ScienceOpen (Lagos, n=437, 2025): **57.2% had only 3 months leave, 46.5% could NOT breastfeed at work, 41.2% had no nearby crèche**, and inability to breastfeed was significantly linked to depression/anxiety (p<0.05).
2. **The restroom-pump reality.** Guardian.ng (2026): Doris Cole, 29-yo Product Manager, resumed at 3 months, *"locked myself in the restroom during lunch breaks… by evening my breasts were painfully engorged, my supply dropped, I blamed myself… crying in the office restroom."* Employers "need lactation rooms, scheduled pumping breaks."
3. **Diaspora African mothers face pump unfamiliarity + stigma.** Springer *Int'l Breastfeeding Journal* (2025, African migrant mothers in US): breast pumps perceived "cumbersome, painful," public breastfeeding stigmatised (acceptable at home, not in US); guilt when unable to meet breastfeeding goals; limited support beyond husband who soon returns to work. NICE NG247 (UK, 2025): women felt **"unprofessional," embarrassed, ashamed washing pump parts**, feared judgement — *"separating the role of mother and employee was difficult."*
4. **Guilt & identity fracture are universal & documented.** LSE research (80 working mothers, incl. Africa): *"I was not giving the best of myself to my baby; I was not giving 100% to my work… a terrible wife, a mediocre mother and not good enough at work."* BBC Worklife: "parental identity threat" → shame → withdrawal from work. Pew Research (2026): **70% of full-time working parents do parenting tasks at work; 81% of working moms; 54% say balancing is difficult; 62% of full-time working moms say it's difficult** (vs 47% dads).
5. **Separation anxiety is neurobiological, not weakness.** Phoenix Health: maternal separation anxiety is real; ~1 in 5 women experience perinatal anxiety; *"the anxiety is proportionate to how much you care, not how ready you are."* Medela: practice separations, brief goodbyes.
6. **Flexibility is a right (UK) / a negotiation (Nigeria).** Acas & Citizens Advice: UK employees can request flexible working from day one; employer cannot fire for the request. Nigerian Labour Act: **two 30-min breastfeeding breaks/day** but enforcement weak.
7. **Cultural layer — omugwo.** PMC (2024): Ọmụgwọ = grandmother postpartum care, 7 wks–6 months unpaid; conflict with mother-in-law common. Naija Feminists (2025): omugwo can become "maternal suffering," PPD 14.6–30% in Nigeria; "strong woman" silence. AJOL: husbands face economic/social pressure during omugwo and *"cultural norms do not allow for complaints."*
8. **Diaspora Black/African mum communities exist & are hungry.** Black Working Mothers (UK), Akwaaba (Ghanaian Twi "welcome"), TheMumPillar (Nigerian-born founder Dr Moyinoluwa Abudu, UK), The Motherhood Group, Black Moms Breastfeeding Uncensored — all signal an organised, ad-responsive audience.
