## RESEARCH BASELINE (Real Demand Signals — Cited)

All niches below are grounded in the following real sources gathered via Agent Reach (Exa was rate-limited; research completed via built-in websearch + Jina Reader + open clinical/peer-reviewed literature).

1. **Prevalence is huge and under-served.** A cross-sectional study in Kano, Nigeria found DR prevalence of **84% in pregnant and 64.7% in postpartum women**, most common at the umbilicus, with prevalence rising with age and parity (Bichi & Hanif, *Diastasis Recti Among Pregnant and Postpartum Women in Kano, Nigeria*, 2017). Globally, DR affects **30–60%+ of postpartum women** (HuffPost 2019; Orange County Plastic Surgery 2026 notes >60% at 6 weeks).
2. **"Still looks pregnant" is a documented, high-emotion pain.** HuffPost (2019) reports women feel they "still look pregnant when their little ones are already out of diapers"; one mother was asked if she was pregnant **three years** after her last baby. Springer medicine confirms the post-pregnancy abdomen "superficially… shows the appearance of still appearing pregnant." MUTU System (Wendy Powell) dedicates a full page to *"Why Your Tummy Still Looks Pregnant… When You're Not!"* listing DR, posture, hormones, weight as causes.
3. **A whole community exists around the shame.** StillLookPregnant.com is a blog built entirely for "every non-pregnant mom who is asked if she is pregnant," with first-person stories of strangers rubbing the belly and asking "when is the baby due," and mothers "running off crying." Reddit r/Mommit thread *"I Look Actually Pregnant and it's Killing Me. I'm a Year…"* (2024) shows sustained anguish at 12+ months PP. Orange County Plastic Surgery (2026) notes Reddit threads *"3 years postpartum and I still look 6 months pregnant"* draw **hundreds of comments and thousands of upvotes**.
4. **Return-to-running is a validated, consensus-driven sub-market.** British Journal of Sports Medicine published two Delphi consensus statements (2023/2024): minimum 3 weeks rest, gradual walk-run progression, running typically 3–6 months postpartum, DR framed as a *fear-avoidance* barrier, and a person-specific timeline. The APTA *Postpartum Return to Running Program* (2023) is a 6-phase protocol — but explicitly "not designed for the layperson to use alone without guidance from a physical therapist." This gap = our opportunity.
5. **"What exercises to AVOID" is the #1 search intent.** Repeated clinical/PT sources (Samson PT; Ashley Gadelha, Pelvic PT; Every Mother; MUTU; Tupler Technique; Legion Athletics; Bethany Hansen PT) converge: avoid crunches, sit-ups, full planks, double leg lifts, breath-holding, and high-impact running/jumping *early*; watch for "doming." The dominant safe progression is: diaphragmatic breathing → TVA activation → pelvic floor → heel slides, toe taps, glute bridges, dead-bug prep, side planks from knees.
6. **Daily baby-care movements are where mothers get re-injured.** DPT Capstone (UNC, 2022, *ergonomicmama*) identifies that pelvic PTs spend the most time teaching *how to move with your baby* (lifting from floor, crib, car seat) to prevent DR/back injury. NHS Lothian DRAM guidelines advise **avoid holding baby on one hip** and use **symmetrical weight-bearing**. Tupler Technique advises bracing the transverse before every lift.
7. **African/Nigerian context is distinctive and underserved.** Nigerian studies show: (a) **61% of postpartum women were unaware they could exercise postpartum** and 47.8% did none (Adeniyi et al., Ibadan 2013); (b) **77.2% owned no exercise equipment** (Ibadan 2013); (c) cultural confinement/"omugwo"-type rest up to 40 days, belly-binding with wrapper, and myths that exercise is unsafe (Mbada et al. 2015; Obio-Cottage Hospital Rivers State 2026 — barriers: financial constraints, lack of female-only/safe spaces, self-consciousness from societal perceptions); (d) higher parity drives DR (Kano 2017); (e) wa3ist-to-hip ratio predicts IRD in Nigerian parous women (Ojukwu et al. 2021); (f) Campus Cybercafe's *"How to Get a Flat Tummy After Delivery in Nigeria"* already monetises this curiosity with belly-binding + DR-safe advice + Nigerian Facebook/WhatsApp groups.
8. **Competitor pricing is 5–13× our price — leaving a low-ticket gap.** MUTU System $132/yr (≈£99, UK-based, 150k+ women, NHS-recognised); Every Mother $55.99/3-mo or $119.40/yr; Tupler Technique $240–$427; Restore Your Core (Lauren Ohayon) 12-wk program, 10k+ women; The Bloom Method, Get Mom Strong comparable. **None are Nigeria/Africa-centred, none are one-time <$10, none speak to omugwo/extended-family/diaspora isolation.** Our N9,800 / $9.97 one-time PDF sits in the empty low-ticket, culturally-native slot.
9. **The 10-minute daily format is proven and demanded.** nourishmovelove's 10-min DR workout (8 exercises × 30s, 2 rounds), Lauren Fitter's "lose your mommy pooch" 10-min, The Bump's 8-exercise DR routine, and Ashley Nowe (Get Mom Strong) recommending 5 min AM/PM breathing — all confirm a short, repeatable, at-home format fits a new mother's life.
