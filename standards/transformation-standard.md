# TRANSFORMATION STANDARD
*Factory standards file · derived from Standard v1 §4–§6, §23–§26, Gates 1–2; record structure in `schemas/transformation.schema.json`.*

## 1 · Definition

The Transformation Specification is the canonical contract defining what transformation exists,
for whom, in what situation, what changes, why it should change, how change is implemented, how
failure is handled, and how success is evidenced. A transformation cannot become a product merely
because the topic is popular.

## 2 · Mandatory situation hierarchy

**Broad topic → Submarket → Specific recurring situation → Specific person → Trigger → Failed
attempt / constraint → Emotional stake → Desired transformation.**

The product title must never be allowed to hide an insufficiently scoped situation.

## 3 · The eleven blocks (each maps to schema fields)

1. **Situation** — all Gate-1 fields non-empty.
2. **Before state** — current behavior/conditions, emotional state, practical consequences,
   failed attempts, environmental constraints, people involved.
3. **After state** — new capabilities, improvements, systems created, **remaining limits**
   (honesty), observable **evidence of change** (≥1 — without it: candidate only).
4. **Mechanism** — core mechanism + supporting mechanisms + why it should work + evidence basis
   with claim-status labels.
5. **Transformation path** — staged sequence with customer actions and required assets. A
   progression, never an information pile.
6. **Failure-point map** — every major failure type carries a concrete rescue protocol.
7. **First win** — within 15 minutes; named action + observable change.
8. **TSM** — see §4.
9. **Maintenance** — maintenance system + relapse protocol + re-entry protocol.
10. **Next transformation** — journey routing; explicit "none yet" reason if empty.
11. **Safety** — risk level, scope boundary (what this does NOT cover), red flags, escalation
    routes to the verified crisis list.

## 4 · TSM standard (§26 — all seven elements mandatory)

1. Baseline 2. Measurable indicators 3. Check-in schedule (default Day 0/7/14/30; another
schedule allowed) 4. Success threshold (**business call — owner approves**) 5. Measurement method
6. Interpretation of incomplete progress 7. Next action on a miss.

TSM is NOT engagement: pages read, videos watched, completion %, downloads and quiz scores are
usage signals, not success. TSM measures actual before→after movement.

## 5 · Status lifecycle

`research → candidate → validated → active → retired`. Only `validated`+ may be referenced by a
product specification.

## 6 · Drift rule (§25)

Before every publish: remove the title, compare actual contents against the approved situation
record. If the content now solves a different problem, the product has drifted — send back.
Examples of drift: wardrobe product silently becoming recovery content; sleep-coordination product
silently becoming mental-health treatment; C-section product silently becoming surgical rehab.

## 7 · Ecosystem rule (§23)

Every transformation must answer: what situation does this solve · what transformation is
delivered · what situation is likely next · what existing transformation naturally leads into it ·
what products must NOT be attached because they solve the same situation.
