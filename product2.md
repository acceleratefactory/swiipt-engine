# Postpartum Product Library — Final Build Set
*Titles follow the agreed naming principle: person + situation + problem + desired outcome first, then a title/subtitle drawn from that sentence — never a category label.*

---

## PHASE 1 — Build & Launch Now

### PRODUCT 1

**Buyer-situation (the research unit, not the title):** Woman 6 weeks–12 months postpartum, confused and afraid that her belly, core, or movement is abnormal; doesn't know what can realistically change at her stage, what's safe to do, or when something needs a doctor instead of a guide.

**Micro-markets folded in:** A1 (C-section shelf) + A2 (diastasis/coning) + B4 (movement fear) + C5 (recovery-timeline anger — "I was told 6 weeks and I'd bounce back")

> ## TITLE: *Is This Normal? Understanding Your Post-Baby Belly, Scar, Core & Recovery Timeline*
> ### SUBTITLE: *What's Actually Happening, What's Safe to Do This Week, and When to See a Doctor*

**Transformation:** Confused and afraid → informed, has a concrete safe-movement plan, knows the red flags that mean "stop, see a doctor," and has realistic expectations for her actual stage of recovery instead of an arbitrary six-week promise.

**Entry-point angles (diagnostic quiz routes her in, content is shared):**
- *"My scar healed. Why does my stomach still look pregnant?"* (A1)
- *"Your belly still bulges when you lift your child — here's why."* (A2)
- *"Nobody told you what's actually safe to do after a C-section."* (B4)
- *"They said six weeks. It's been longer. Here's what's actually normal."* (C5)

---

### PRODUCT 2

**Buyer-situation:** Woman 3–9 months postpartum whose old wardrobe technically fits but doesn't suit her changed proportions; loses time every morning to rejected outfits; done waiting to "get her body back" before she gets dressed properly again.

**Micro-markets folded in:** D1 (everyday wardrobe) + D3 (return-to-work) + styling implications of A3/A5/A6 (loose skin, breast changes, marks — addressed as *dressing for* these, not fixing them)

> ## TITLE: *The New-Body Closet: Dressing the Shape You Have Now — Not the One You're Waiting For*
> ### SUBTITLE: *A Practical Wardrobe System for Postpartum Nigerian Women Who Are Done Waiting to "Get Their Body Back"*

**Transformation:** Frustrated and stuck in leggings out of decision fatigue → knows how to dress her current body, builds outfits quickly, feels put-together for work, errands, and everyday life.

## PHASE 1.5 — Upsell (attached to Product 2, not a standalone launch)

**Buyer-situation:** A Product 2 customer with a wedding, ceremony, or work event weeks away, outfit or fabric already committed to, no time to change her body before it happens.

**Micro-market:** D2 (event dressing)

> ## TITLE: *The 3-Week Notice: Look Put-Together for the Event You Can't Skip*
> ### SUBTITLE: *Make the Outfit You Already Have Work Beautifully — No Crash Diet Required*

**Where it lives:** Post-purchase offer to Product 2 buyers only. Not built or marketed as a separate acquisition product. Whether it earns standalone status later depends on how many Product 2 buyers take it.

## PHASE 2 — Build the asset now, launch after Wave 1 has customers

**Buyer-situation:** Woman 6 weeks–12 months postpartum living around urine leakage — carrying pads everywhere, avoiding exercise, anxious about prayer, or having quietly decided this is just what motherhood does to a body — and hasn't told anyone.

**Micro-markets folded in:** B1-A (startle leak) + B1-B (exercise leak) + B1-C (prayer/purity leak) + B1-D (normalized leak) — one product, four entry doors.

> ## TITLE: *The Quiet Leak: Taking Back Control of Your Body After Baby*
> ### SUBTITLE: *A Private, Evidence-Based Guide to Understanding and Improving Postpartum Bladder Control*

**Transformation:** Quietly living around the problem, or believing it's simply permanent → understands it deserves attention, has a safe pelvic-floor progression to follow, and knows when to seek professional assessment.

**Distribution plan (deliberately not Meta cold-ads):** WhatsApp groups, trusted community voices, referral from Product 1/2 buyers, private recommendation. Content and four landing pages get built during Wave 1 so it's ready to launch the moment there's a warm audience to send it to.

# Postpartum Micro-Market Breakdown — Market Reality + Library Role
*Two separate classifications, applied in order. Nothing is discarded; nothing is finalized as one SKU.*

**Classification 1 — Market Reality:** what's actually true about the buyer and the problem, independent of what we build.
**Classification 2 — Library Role:** what this micro-market can *become* — a micro-market can carry several roles at once (module + ad angle + lead-magnet source), and a role assignment here is a hypothesis, not a final product decision.

---

## BRANCH A — Shape & Appearance

### A1 — C-section shelf
**Buyer-situation:** Woman 8–16 weeks after C-section, incision healed, persistent bulge above the scar, afraid exercising or lifting her baby will "damage something inside."

| Pain | Urgency | Active-seeking | Existing spend/workaround | Competitor | Cultural gap | Ad-safety/Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| High | Medium (grows if unresolved) | **High** — ranks organically ("bulge above C-section scar") | Belly bands, binders (proven spend) | None paid; free clinical blog content only | Full — no Nigerian source addresses this | Medium-High — reads as recovery/fitness |

**Library roles:** Flagship component (hypothesis) · Standalone-possible on its own · Lead-magnet source ("Is your C-section shelf normal? 3-question check") · Ad angle ("My scar healed. Why does my stomach still look pregnant?") · Cross-sell into wardrobe (D1)

---

### A2 — Diastasis / coning
**Buyer-situation:** Woman 4–12 months postpartum whose belly domes or cones when she sits up, lifts her toddler, or exercises; tried generic ab workouts that made it worse; afraid normal movement is causing damage.

| Pain | Urgency | Active-seeking | Existing spend/workaround | Competitor | Cultural gap | Ad-safety/Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| High | Medium | High — "coning," "doming" are real search terms | Some (tried workouts, no purchase) | **Direct competitor exists**: $14.99 generic Western Gumroad guide | Full — no African/Nigerian version, no functional-motherhood framing | Medium-High |

**Library roles:** Flagship component (hypothesis) · Standalone-possible if differentiated on Nigerian-functional-motherhood angle (lifting toddler, market trading, carrying) rather than generic "close your gap" · Module inside A1 if bundling validates · Ad angle ("Your belly still bulges when you lift your child — here's why")

---

### A3 — Apron belly / loose skin
**Buyer-situation:** Woman 6–18 months postpartum who has lost most pregnancy weight but has a hanging lower-belly pouch; doesn't know what's fixable vs. what she needs to dress around.

| Pain | Urgency | Active-seeking | Existing spend/workaround | Competitor | Cultural gap | Ad-safety/Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Med-High | Low | Medium | High — creams, wraps, detox teas (crowded *physical*-product space) | Physical-product market saturated; no info product | Some | Medium — risk of "inferiority" language re: loose skin |

**Library roles:** Module (inside A1/A2 flagship, "what won't fully close, and how to dress around it") · Not standalone — buyer already spends on physical products, not information · Ad angle only if reframed around realistic-expectations, not "fix this"

---

### A4 — Evening bloat/distension
**Buyer-situation:** Woman 2–6 months pp, flat-ish in the morning, visibly distended by evening; tried dietary restriction/teas without understanding cause.

| Pain | Urgency | Active-seeking | Existing spend/workaround | Competitor | Cultural gap | Ad-safety/Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medium | Medium (daily annoyance) | Low-Medium | Teas, restriction — **scam-adjacent space** | High saturation (detox/wellness scams) | Low differentiation available | Medium |

**Library roles:** Bonus chapter only · Not standalone — real risk of reading as another scam · Could be an email/WhatsApp nurture piece once trust is established, not a cold-traffic angle

---

### A5 — Breasts after weaning
**Buyer-situation:** Woman 1–12 months post-weaning whose breasts changed shape/volume; stopped wearing certain necklines or swimwear; hasn't discussed it with a partner.

| Pain | Urgency | Active-seeking | Existing spend/workaround | Competitor | Cultural gap | Ad-safety/Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Med-High | Low | Low — private, not typically searched | Bras (some spend) | Low | Some | **Careful** — "sagging"/negative body-part language risks Meta's inferiority-statement ban |

**Library roles:** Standalone possible if reframed around bra-fit/necklines rather than negative appearance language · Module inside identity companion layer · Ad angle needs testing for policy compliance before spend

---

### A6 — Stretch marks / loose-skin intimacy avoidance
**Buyer-situation:** Woman postpartum avoiding undressing in front of a partner or swimming with friends because of marks/texture on hips or thighs.

**Library roles:** Module inside A5 or C-branch companion layer · Not standalone — same physical-product-competes-with-info-product issue as A3 (oils/creams already own this spend)

## BRANCH B — Physical Recovery

### B1-A — Startle leakage (sneeze/laugh/cough)
**Buyer-situation:** Woman 6 weeks–6 months pp who leaks on sneeze/laugh/cough; carries pads everywhere, afraid of being noticed.

| Pain | Urgency | Active-seeking | Existing spend/workaround | Competitor | Cultural gap | Ad-safety/Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Very High | High (daily) | **Low** — stigma suppresses even searching | Pads only | None paid, anywhere | Full | **Low** — documented near-total Meta ad rejection for this category |

**Library roles:** Standalone product (content is genuinely ready to build) · Distribution must be organic/WhatsApp/community-referral, not cold Meta ads · Possible partnership angle (a trusted mum-influencer or community voice, not a paid ad)

### B1-B — Exercise/activity leakage
**Buyer-situation:** Woman 3–12 months pp who leaks during running/jumping/dance and has quit the activity rather than solve the leak.
**Reality:** Same core pain profile as B1-A, but the emotional register is *lost activity/frustration* rather than *fear of discovery* — different ad language, same underlying product likely serves both.
**Library roles:** Ad-angle variant of B1 standalone, not a separate product · Landing-page segment

### B1-C — Prayer/purity leakage
**Buyer-situation:** Muslim woman postpartum experiencing leakage, anxious about ritual cleanliness for prayer specifically.
**Reality:** Distinct emotional register (religious/purity, not just embarrassment) — this is the segment most likely to be actively discussing the problem within trusted community circles (mosque women's groups) even while avoiding public search — meaning **community distribution may work especially well here**, even though open search doesn't.
**Library roles:** Distinct landing page / WhatsApp sequence · Same core product · High-trust community partnership candidate

### B1-D — Normalized leakage
**Buyer-situation:** Mother who leaks lifting her baby/toddler and has stopped seeing it as a problem — believes it's just "what motherhood does."
**Reality:** Different psychology entirely — she isn't shopping, she needs to be told this is treatable before she'll buy anything. Weakest direct-response angle of the four, strongest educational/awareness angle.
**Library roles:** Top-of-funnel educational content (not an ad, a piece of shareable content) that feeds B1-A/B/C · Not a standalone purchase trigger on its own

### B2 — Prolapse
**Buyer-situation:** Woman postpartum with dragging heaviness or a felt bulge, hasn't told anyone, believes something is "wrong with her."

| Pain | Urgency | Active-seeking | Existing spend/workaround | Competitor | Cultural gap | Ad-safety/Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Very High | High | Low (shame, fear) | None | None | Full | Low — same ad-rejection pattern as B1, higher medical-trust bar on top |

**Library roles:** Hold — real, but needs clinical-review credibility infrastructure and a non-ads distribution plan before building. Revisit once B1's community-distribution model is proven.

### B4 — C-section movement fear
**Buyer-situation:** Woman 6–12 weeks post-C-section, healed but afraid to twist/bend/lift; wants a concrete "what's safe this week" answer.
**Reality:** Overlaps heavily with A1's buyer — same clinical stage, same underlying fear, different surface complaint (fear vs. visible bulge).
**Library roles:** Flagship component (hypothesis, shared with A1) · Standalone-possible independently if A1 bundling doesn't validate · Worksheet ("what's safe this week") usable inside either version

### B5 — Carrying-related back pain
**Buyer-situation:** Mother 3–12 months pp whose back worsens from carrying/feeding/settling baby; avoiding household or income tasks because of it.

| Pain | Urgency | Active-seeking | Existing spend/workaround | Competitor | Cultural gap | Ad-safety/Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Med-High | High (income impact for traders) | Medium | Painkillers, ignoring it | **Very high saturation** — free PT/physio content everywhere | Some (market-trading/carrying-goods angle is Nigeria-specific) | Medium-High |

**Library roles:** Module/upsell after flagship trust is built, not a cold-traffic standalone (too crowded to win on cold ads against established PT brands) · Possible standalone later if positioned specifically around income-generating carrying (market women, not generic "mum back pain")

### B7 — Perineal/intimacy pain
**Buyer-situation:** Woman 3–6 months pp after a tear, sex still painful, avoiding her husband, afraid something is wrong.
**Reality:** Same ad-rejection pattern as B1/B2, highest privacy sensitivity on the list.
**Library roles:** Hold for paid-ads distribution · Possible private/organic-only offer (email list, not cold traffic) later

### B9 — Functional strength (can't lift toddler)
**Buyer-situation:** Mother of 2+ young children whose belly bulges or back gives out lifting her older child; reads to her as maternal failure, not just fitness.
**Reality:** Not a distinct buyer from A2/B5 — this is a *feeling*, not a separate physical complaint.
**Library roles:** Marketing angle only, folded into A2/flagship messaging and B5 — not a separate research unit going forward

### B8, B10 — Bowel/hemorrhoid issues, exhaustion
**Library roles:** Bonus-gift tier only, unchanged from original report's own verdict — no new evidence moves these

## BRANCH C — Body Identity

### C1 — Photo/mirror avoidance
**Buyer-situation:** Woman who takes family photos but immediately says "please don't post that one" — a specific, observable behavior, not a mood.
**Reality:** Free therapist/counselor content saturates this space (their lead-gen, not a competing product), so no direct paid competitor — but also no tangible ₦9,800 mechanism on its own.
**Library roles:** **Strong acquisition angle**, independent of whether it ever becomes a product — "Why do you love the family photo until you see yourself in it?" · Worksheet (photo-confidence exercise) as a bundle component · Email sequence into flagship or D1 · Not a standalone SKU

### C2 — Social comparison (Instagram snap-back)
**Reality:** Same pattern as C1 — real, free-content-saturated, better as an angle than a product.
**Library roles:** Ad angle / module inside a "realistic recovery timeline" chapter attached to the flagship

### C3 — Partner-attraction fear
**Reality:** Genuine pain, but ad copy implying knowledge of a relationship problem is precisely what Meta's "personal attributes" detection flags.
**Library roles:** Module only, inside identity companion layer · Do not use as a cold-ad angle — test very carefully if used at all

### C5 — Recovery-timeline anger
**Library roles:** Module (realistic-expectations chapter) inside flagship — pairs naturally with the "is this normal" framing already in A1/A2/B4

### C6 — Multi-birth body grief
**Library roles:** Module, later-stage content (2+ children) — not early-funnel, better suited to an email sequence for repeat customers

---

## BRANCH D — Function / Reintegration

### D1 — Everyday wardrobe
**Buyer-situation:** Woman 3–9 months pp whose clothes technically fit but don't suit her changed proportions; loses time each morning to rejected outfits.

| Pain | Urgency | Active-seeking | Existing spend/workaround | Competitor | Cultural gap | Ad-safety/Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medium | Low (chronic, not acute) | Low (browsing) | Fashion spend generally | Saturated globally, open on Nigeria/Ankara specifics | Full | **Highest safety on the entire list** |

**Library roles:** Standalone (second build) · Cross-sell target for flagship buyers · Safest category to test ad creative and audiences on generally, even before deciding it's "the" second product

### D2 — Event dressing (renamed from "owambe")
**Buyer-situation:** Woman with a wedding/ceremony/work event weeks away, outfit/fabric already committed to, no time to diet.
**Reality:** Occupied on the diet mechanism ("The Owambe Body" exists and appears to be working) — open on the "dress it now, don't diet it" mechanism.
**Library roles:** Upsell to D1, not a standalone cold launch · Time-boxed ad angle usable seasonally (wedding season, December owambe season)

### D3 — Return-to-work wardrobe
**Library roles:** Module/variant of D1, not a separate research unit

### D4 — Old-jeans ritual
**Buyer-situation:** Woman who keeps a specific pre-baby item as a self-worth benchmark, tries it on periodically, hasn't decided to let it go.
**Library roles:** **Lead magnet** — "The Old Jeans Test" — pulls in C1/C2/C6 buyers before they've found a product yet, feeds into D1 or the flagship

## Open Hypotheses (not decisions — next validation step)

1. **Does A1+A2+B4 belong in one flagship, or are they three adjacent products that bundle later?** Only real buyer-language research (do the same women mention shelf + coning + movement-fear in the same post, or do they show up as separate conversations?) resolves this.
2. **Does B1's four sub-angles (A/B/C/D) need four landing pages feeding one product, or four distinct products?** Testable cheaply once B1 building starts — same content, different front doors.
3. **Is D1 genuinely a second build, or does it work better as month-2 cross-sell only after the flagship has an email list to sell into?** Affects whether to build both now or sequence them.

Nothing above is closed. This is the map to validate against, not the final architecture.
