## 01 — PRODUCT IDENTITY
- **Product name:** The 3-Week Notice (Look Put-Together for the Event You Can't Skip)
- **Life area:** m01 Postpartum (wardrobe/appearance sub-theme — same domain as PP-02, not the work/admin domain)
- **Life state(s):** postpartum-body-recovery, upcoming-event
- **Trigger(s):** wedding-invitation / naming-ceremony / owambe / work-event-with-short-notice
- **Library tier:** Active System — **upsell only**, offered post-purchase to PP-02 buyers. Not built or marketed as a standalone acquisition product (per product2.md's original decision — whether it earns standalone status later depends on how many PP-02 buyers take it).
- **Entry doors → one transformation:** All variants of the same situation — "I have an event in weeks, an outfit or fabric already committed to, and no time or ability to change my body before it happens."
- **Target person:** A PP-02 (New-Body Closet) customer who now has a specific event — wedding, naming ceremony, owambe, a work event — within roughly 3–6 weeks, whose outfit or fabric is already chosen/committed (often Aso-Ebi, already-tailored, or already-owned), and who is anxious about how she'll look in it.
- **Situation:** When a postpartum mother has an unavoidable event weeks away and an outfit already committed to, she struggles to feel presentable because her body has changed since the outfit was chosen and there's no time left to change it back, and wants to make what she already has work — not a crash diet.
- **Problem:** Time-boxed appearance anxiety about a specific already-chosen garment; social pressure at Nigerian events (naming ceremonies, owambe, church) around looking "recovered."
- **Constraints:** Fixed outfit (often can't be exchanged — fabric already cut/tailored, Aso-Ebi already bought), fixed date (can't be moved), no realistic time for body change, may have limited budget for last-minute alterations.
- **Desired transformation:** Walks into the event looking and feeling put-together in the outfit she already has, without having attempted or failed at a crash diet in the meantime.

## 02 — BEFORE STATE
- Experiencing: dread counting down to the event date; outfit doesn't sit the way it used to over the changed waist/belly.
- Doing now: trying to lose weight fast, avoiding trying the outfit on until the last possible moment, considering shapewear that may be uncomfortable or unsafe this early postpartum.
- Not working: crash dieting in 3 weeks (doesn't work, adds stress on top of postpartum recovery).
- Emotional: dread, self-consciousness, fear of comments from relatives ("you've let yourself go") at the event itself.
- Practical: outfit already paid for/tailored/committed to (often Aso-Ebi with a fixed group), can't simply buy something else without cost or logistics.
- Failed attempts: bought a size down hoping to "grow into it" by the date; tried a quick diet; avoided trying the outfit on out of fear of confirming it doesn't fit.
- Environmental: Nigerian event culture (naming ceremonies, owambe, church) where being seen and photographed is part of the occasion, sometimes with in-law/community scrutiny.
- People involved: the event's Aso-Ebi group/family (fixed fabric/style), possibly husband or in-laws commenting on appearance.

## 03 — AFTER STATE
- Can now: make the already-committed outfit sit well on her current shape without altering the garment's core identity (color/fabric/group match preserved).
- Easier: attending the event without dreading being photographed.
- Changed: outfit fits comfortably (adapted, not forced); she has 1–2 quick no-sew or minimal-alteration adjustments ready.
- Reduced: last-minute panic; comment-related anxiety at the event.
- System now: a garment-adaptation checklist + a "what to do this week" 3-week countdown.
- Evidence: outfit adapted and tried on with a fit she's satisfied with before the event; has at least one response ready for an appearance-related comment.

## 04 — TRANSFORMATION MECHANISM
- **M1 Assess the garment, not the body:** What can flex (waist ties, wrap styles, layering) vs. what's fixed (a tailored, fitted piece) — start from what the outfit allows, not from a body-change goal.
- **M2 Adapt with minimal, fast interventions:** No-sew/quick-alteration options (belts, wraps, strategic layering, a tailor visit for a specific let-out seam) matched to how much time is actually left.
- **M3 Protect comfort, not just appearance:** C-section scar sensitivity, breastfeeding access, and general postpartum comfort factored into every adaptation suggestion — this is not generic styling advice.
- **M4 Script the moment:** A short, ready response for the "you haven't lost the weight" comment, so the event itself doesn't become another source of shame.
- Why each matters: starting from the garment (not the body) makes the 3-week timeline actually achievable; fast interventions match the real time constraint; comfort-first prevents recommending something that will hurt a still-healing body; the script protects her at the one moment she can't avoid — the event itself.

## 05 — TRANSFORMATION PATH
Assess the outfit (what flexes, what's fixed) → Week 1: quick fixes + tailor decision if needed → Week 2: trial fit + adjust → Week 3: final fit + comfort check → Event day: dressing sequence + comment script ready → Post-event: what to do with the outfit/lessons for next time.

## 06 — ASSET MAP (READ / DO / DECIDE / COMMUNICATE)
- READ: Short guide "The 3-Week Notice" (functional-first, lighter than a full flipbook magazine — this is a fast upsell, not a flagship read).
- DO: Garment-adaptation checklist (what to check, what to try first).
- DECIDE: "Adapt it yourself / take it to a tailor / borrow-and-return" decision tree based on days remaining and garment type.
- COMMUNICATE: The appearance-comment response script (for relatives/in-laws at the event).
- TRACK: 3-week countdown checklist (not a long-term tracker — this product has a hard deadline).
- RECOVER: "It's 5 days out and nothing's worked yet" emergency card (fastest possible fixes).

## 07 — FAILURE-POINT MAP
- Normal: runs out of time for a planned tailor visit → emergency 5-day card.
- Bad day: tries the outfit on and it's worse than expected → reassurance + the "adapt, don't panic" reset.
- Missed: forgets to start in week 1 → compressed 1-week version of the same steps.
- Resistance: family pressure about appearance before she's ready to deal with it → the comment script, used proactively.
- Unexpected: garment genuinely doesn't work even adapted → "borrow or alternative outfit" fallback path, without framing it as failure.
- Overwhelm: too many options presented → narrow to the single fastest fix for her specific garment type.

## 08 — FIRST-WIN DESIGN
Within 15 minutes: try the outfit on, identify using the checklist exactly which 1–2 things aren't sitting right, and get the single fastest fix for that specific issue (e.g., a wrap-tie adjustment). Immediate, concrete relief that the outfit is fixable, not a failure.

## 09 — TSM (TRANSFORMATION SUCCESS MODEL)
- outfit tried on and adaptation plan chosen within week 1
- at least one adaptation completed before the event
- self-rated readiness for the event up (before vs. days-before comparison)
- comment-response script reviewed/practiced
