## 01 — PRODUCT IDENTITY
- **Product name:** Is This Normal? (Postpartum Body, Scar & Core Recovery Reorientation)
- **Life area:** m01 Postpartum
- **Life state(s):** early-to-mid-postpartum (6 weeks–12 months), physical recovery from birth
- **Trigger(s):** c-section-scar-confusion / diastasis-coning / movement-fear / recovery-timeline-anger
- **Library tier:** Active System (Library Wave 1)
- **Strategic note:** This product is a **deliberate consolidation** of two independently-validated flagships from the 21-product gate — V03 Diastasis/Core Recovery (score 86) and V05 C-Section Recovery (score 80) — into one physical-recovery product, on the hypothesis that the same woman asks all four questions in the same conversation. This consolidation is **officially confirmed** for the Phase 1 build and launch. If buyer-language research or split-testing later shows C-section-scar buyers and diastasis buyers are actually two separate conversations, they can be split back into two products.
- **Four entry doors → one transformation (all four = physical recovery uncertainty, not four different life problems):**
  - **A1** — "My scar healed. Why does my stomach still look pregnant?" (C-section shelf/scar)
  - **A2** — "My belly still bulges when I lift my child" (diastasis/coning)
  - **B4** — "Nobody told me what's actually safe to do after a C-section" (movement fear)
  - **C5** — "They said six weeks. It's been longer. What's actually normal?" (recovery-timeline anger)
- **What this product deliberately does NOT cover** (do not re-absorb these — they are separately validated products):
  - Emotional volatility, crying, numbness, rage → **V07** ("I Love My Baby, Why Am I Empty?")
  - Partner/relationship strain → **V11** ("We Became Roommates")
  - General household/routine overwhelm not tied to physical recovery → **V06/V09/V16** depending on the specific overwhelm
  - If a buyer's real question is emotional or relational, this product should hand off to V07/V11, not attempt to answer it inside a physical-recovery frame.
- **Target person:** Mother 6 weeks–12 months postpartum, confused and afraid that her belly, scar, or core is healing abnormally; doesn't know what's realistic for her actual stage, what's safe to attempt, or when a symptom needs a doctor instead of a guide.
- **Situation:** When a postpartum mother sees or feels physical changes in her belly, scar, or core that don't match what she expected, she struggles to tell normal recovery from a warning sign because she has no reference frame for her specific stage, and wants a concrete, stage-accurate answer plus a safe-movement plan.
- **Problem:** Cannot distinguish normal physical recovery from something requiring medical attention; DIY fixes (situps, generic YouTube routines) risk making diastasis worse; the "six weeks and you'll bounce back" myth sets an unrealistic timeline that fuels shame.
- **Constraints:** Exhausted, time-poor, often can't afford a physiotherapist, may not know self-tests exist or how to do them correctly, low bandwidth for long reading.
- **Desired transformation:** Confidently knows "this is normal for my stage / this needs a call," has a safe, stage-appropriate movement plan, and has a realistic recovery timeline instead of a myth to be ashamed of missing.

## 02 — BEFORE STATE
- Experiencing: visible belly bulge/coning when lifting, scar tightness or numbness, uncertainty about what's healed vs. still healing.
- Doing now: Googling symptoms at 3am, comparing her belly to friends', trying sit-ups or generic ab workouts, avoiding movement out of fear.
- Not working: Generic "get your body back" content that doesn't triage by recovery stage; sit-ups/crunches that actively worsen diastasis.
- Emotional: Anxious, ashamed, afraid she's "doing it wrong" or that the damage is permanent.
- Practical: Skips exercise entirely (deconditioning) or does the wrong exercise (worsening it) — no safe middle path offered anywhere she's looked.
- Failed attempts: Read the hospital discharge leaflet once (generic, not stage-specific); tried a YouTube ab workout that made the bulge worse; was told "it'll go by itself" by a relative.
- Environmental: Baby dictates her schedule; may not have physio access or budget.
- People involved: Sometimes a dismissive relative ("it will tighten with time"); rarely a clinician unless something visibly alarms her.

## 03 — AFTER STATE
- Can now: Self-assess her diastasis with the correct finger-width test; know which of her symptoms are expected-for-stage vs. escalate-now.
- Easier: Deciding whether today's twinge/bulge/scar sensation is worth a call.
- Changed: Replaced the "six weeks" myth with a realistic, stage-accurate expectation; stopped doing the exercises that were making it worse.
- Reduced: Anxiety from not knowing; risk of self-worsening the separation via unsafe DIY exercise.
- System now: A 12-week safe core-and-scar progression with a weekly self-check and clear escalation triggers.
- Evidence: Completed self-assessment; following the correct-stage exercise tier; correctly identified at least one real escalation trigger if it occurred; reports lower anxiety about her physical recovery specifically.

## 04 — TRANSFORMATION MECHANISM
- **M1 Normalise (by stage):** Map what's actually expected at 6wk / 3mo / 6mo / 12mo, so she calibrates against her real timeline, not a universal "six weeks" myth.
- **M2 Assess correctly:** Teach the finger-width diastasis self-test properly (as a *screen*, not a diagnosis — sensitivity caveat included) and a scar-healing check.
- **M3 Progress safely:** A 12-week, stage-gated movement plan (breath/TVA activation → functional core → return to normal load) — explicit "do NOT do" list (situps, heavy twists, front-loading) with the reason why.
- **M4 Escalate correctly:** Red-flag list (≥3 finger-widths separation, pain, any prolapse symptom, wound issues) → routes to a real professional, not further self-management.
- Why each matters: normalising by stage kills the shame from the false six-week promise; correct assessment prevents both false alarm and missed danger; safe progression replaces harmful DIY advice with something that actually works; escalation protects against the one failure mode a PDF cannot safely handle alone.

## 05 — TRANSFORMATION PATH
Diagnose (what stage am I actually at) → Normalise (what's expected at this stage) → Assess (self-test, correctly done) → Progress (12-week safe movement plan, stage-gated) → Review (week 6, week 12) → Escalate if needed → Maintain.

## 06 — ASSET MAP (READ / DO / DECIDE / COMMUNICATE)
- READ: **Swipeable flipbook magazine** "Is This Normal?" — stage-by-stage physical recovery explainer (belly/scar/core only).
- DO: 12-week movement progression tracker + weekly self-check.
- DECIDE: Red/Amber/Green triage card for physical symptoms specifically (scar, bulge, pain, bleeding) — NOT a general mood or relationship triage.
- COMMUNICATE: "How to ask your doctor/midwife about this" script — for physical follow-up appointments specifically.
- TRACK: 12-week core/scar progression tracker.
- RECOVER: "Bad day" reset card (pain flare, deconditioning after a missed week).

## 07 — FAILURE-POINT MAP
- Normal failure: skips the weekly check → gentle reminder nudge, not guilt.
- Bad day: scar or core pain flares → rest + heat card, drop back one tier.
- Missed week: gap in the 12-week plan → re-entry protocol, resume at the tier she left off, don't restart from zero.
- Resistance: family says "it'll go by itself" → a short "why this needs more than time" note she can show them.
- Unexpected: separation appears to be worsening, or a red-flag symptom appears → triage card front-and-centre, explicit stop-and-escalate instruction.
- Relapse: goes back to avoiding movement out of fear → re-open the "what's actually safe" section.
- Overwhelm: 12 weeks feels like too much → "Start Here: this week's one exercise" minimum-viable version.
- Restart: stopped for weeks → 3-step re-entry, re-assess stage before resuming (don't assume she's still where she left off).

## 08 — FIRST-WIN DESIGN
Within 15 minutes: correctly perform the finger-width self-test, get a clear "this is within the expected range for your stage" or "this warrants a professional look" answer, and learn the one thing to stop doing this week (usually sit-ups). Immediate clarity, immediate risk-reduction.

## 10 — RE-ENTRY / MAINTENANCE
- Maintenance: monthly self-check for 6 months post-completion; re-test if a new pregnancy or major exertion occurs.
- Relapse: separation reappears or worsens → re-triage, likely escalate.
- Restart: missed streak → 3-step re-entry, re-assess before resuming.
- Next-level: PP-02 (New-Body Closet) once physical recovery has stabilised — dressing the body, not fixing it.
