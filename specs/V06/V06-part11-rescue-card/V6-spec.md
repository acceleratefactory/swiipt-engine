04 — TRANSFORMATION MECHANISM

Why should this work if she follows the system?

Removes the emotional labor of asking. A written roster converts a nightly negotiation (which she loses because she's too tired to fight) into a pre-agreed default. No one has to ask; the schedule asks.
Reframes the partner's role from "helper" to "co-manager." In a status-conscious culture, "help me" invites minimization; "this is your shift" invites ownership. The mechanism is a status upgrade, not a request for charity.
Solves the breastfeeding math honestly. The contract doesn't pretend a breastfeeding mother can be "off duty" all night — it schedules milk hand-off (pumped bottle, or partner brings baby to her for feeding and returns baby to crib) so her sleep opportunity is real, not fictional.
Externalizes the fairness check via a Sleep Banker (Recovery Bank). A credit/debit system tracks night blocks (1 credit = covering a 3–5 hour night block allowing the other to sleep) and recovery paybacks (1 payback = morning sleep-in or 3-hour afternoon nap). All balances are logged and must be redeemed within 7 days to prevent resentment compounding.
Pre-negotiates the resistant partner's objections with ready scripts, so the first use of the contract doesn't require her to win an argument while sleep-deprived.
05 — TRANSFORMATION PATH
Diagnose → Choose Model → Draft Contract → Negotiate → Run It → Review → Reinforce

Day 1 — Diagnose: Map the current (broken) night reality: who wakes, how often, who does what, how many total protected hours per night, how she currently feels by 6am.

Day 2 — Choose your model: Three contract templates by situation —

Template A: Breastfeeding-compatible split shift (partner covers settle/soothe/nappy, hands baby to her for feeds only, returns baby to crib; partner remains aware and alert during their shift; mother is off-duty)
Template B: Formula/expressed-milk split shift (true alternating shifts, one parent fully off)
Roster C: If You Don't Have a Partner Available (Support-Web Roster) (redirects available "village" — relative, paid help, friend — into a scheduled night-adjacent role, e.g. a morning-relief slot)

Day 3 — Draft the contract: Fill in actual clock times, whose "shift" starts/ends when, what counts as an emergency override.

Days 4–5 — Negotiate: Use the provided scripts to present it as a system, not a complaint. Handle the predictable objections ("I have work tomorrow," "I don't hear the baby").

Days 6–12 — Run it: Use the contract for a full week including one weekend "sleep bank" catch-up.

Day 13 — Review: What broke? What worked? Renegotiate using the weekly check-in template.

Days 14+ — Reinforce: Lock in the working version; revisit every 2 weeks as the baby's sleep changes.
