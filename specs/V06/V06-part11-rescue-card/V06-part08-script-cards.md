# V06 — Part 8: The Seven Script Cards
## Every Night, Just Me
### A postpartum night-coordination system that replaces nightly arguing with a pre-agreed roster, a fairness ledger, and the exact words to make it stick.

> **Product:** V06 | **Module:** 8 of 13 | **Type:** Communication Tools / Script Cards
> **Evidence label:** "Educational content informed by relationship research, postpartum health guidance, and lived experience. It is not a substitute for medical, clinical, or mental-health assessment."

---

These scripts are not suggestions. They are precision tools — written for the specific moment, with the specific words that do not trigger defensiveness. Do not improvise at 3 AM. Use the script.

---

## SCRIPT CARD 1: Initiating the Shift Conversation

**When to use:** During the day — never at night — when the baby is calm. To introduce the contract system without making it feel like an attack.

> *"I love how hard we are both working to take care of [Baby's Name]. But my current exhaustion isn't sustainable — I am hitting a wall where I cannot function safely during the day. I want us to look at this night-shift contract template together. It's not about finding fault or blaming you. It's about mapping out a roster so we both get a guaranteed sleep opportunity block. Can we look at it for 5 minutes after lunch?"*

---

## SCRIPT CARD 2: Defusing "I Have Work Tomorrow"

**When to use:** When your partner objects that they need sleep because of their job the next day.

> *"I hear you, and your job is crucial for our family. But taking care of a newborn all day is also a full-time, high-stakes job. If I collapse from exhaustion, drop the baby, or my health fails, our whole household goes down. We both have demanding jobs now — mine starts at sunrise, yours starts at office hours. We both need a minimum safety block of sleep to do our jobs safely. Let's look at the roster template, which protects your sleep for the second half of the night."*

---

## SCRIPT CARD 3: Handling "You Do It Better" (Competence Weaponization)

**When to use:** When your partner claims they don't know how to soothe the baby, or that the baby only calms for you — as a way to avoid night duty.

> *"The baby only prefers me because I've had more hours of practice. You are just as capable of learning how to soothe [Baby's Name]. If I step in every time they cry, you will never get that practice, and I will never get sleep. I am going to put on my white noise machine. I trust you to find your own rhythm with the baby. I'm going to sleep now."*

---

## SCRIPT CARD 4: The Emergency Handoff (The 2 A.M. Valve)

**When to use:** When the active shift manager has reached their limit — the baby won't settle, safety is at risk, you cannot go further.

> *"I have reached my limit. I am holding the baby safely but I am too exhausted to continue safely. I need to activate the emergency handoff. I am putting the baby safely in the crib now and I need you to take over. I'll be back in 30 minutes."*

---

## SCRIPT CARD 5: The 5-Minute Sunday Roster Review

**When to use:** Every Sunday afternoon to adjust the roster for the coming week. Non-negotiable weekly ritual — 5 minutes, no longer.

> *"Let's do our 5-minute night review. How did last week's shifts feel? Did you get your sleep opportunity blocks? The baby is waking up at different times now — do we need to adjust our transition time? Let's write the updated hours on the chart for this week."*

---

## SCRIPT CARD 6: The No-Blame Reset (After a Failed Night)

**When to use:** The morning after a night where the roster collapsed, arguments happened, or someone slept through a shift.

> *"Last night was rough and we both lost our tempers. Let's not blame each other — exhaustion does that to everyone. The roster didn't work because [specific reason: handoff alarm didn't go off / baby was sick]. Let's adjust the system: tonight, we will [specific fix]. We reset today with a clean slate."*

---

## SCRIPT CARD 7: Defusing Extended Family / Omugwo Critique

**When to use:** When mothers, mothers-in-law, or relatives criticize your partner's involvement in night care — saying it is "not a man's job" or that night duty belongs to the woman.

> *"Thank you for your care and concern. This is how we protect our marriage and our baby's safety. Sharing night shifts ensures [Partner's Name] builds a strong bond with the baby from the beginning, and it allows me to heal physically from childbirth. This system works for our household, and we are committed to it."*
