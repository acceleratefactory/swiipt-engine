## What This Page Is For

This night-shift system is designed to protect your sleep opportunity and reduce household conflict. It does that well.

What it cannot do is treat a clinical condition. Severe, sustained exhaustion can mask or worsen postpartum mental health conditions — and a better roster will not fix that. This page helps you recognise when something more is happening, and routes you to appropriate support.

---

## The Exhaustion vs. Clinical Warning Checklist

Review this list during your Sunday Roster Review, or any time something feels different from ordinary tiredness. 

**If you experience two or more of the following consistently for more than two weeks, do not attempt to manage this with sleep schedules alone. Seek professional assessment.**

- [ ] You feel an overwhelming sense of hopelessness, emptiness, or sadness that does not lift even after a good sleep block.
- [ ] You feel intense anxiety, panic, or rapid heartbeats when thinking about the baby's sleep or night-time.
- [ ] You are unable to fall asleep even when the baby is sleeping and your partner is on duty.
- [ ] You experience intrusive, frightening thoughts of harm coming to yourself or the baby.
- [ ] You feel disconnected from your baby, or feel as though they would be better off without you.

## What to Do If You Identify These Symptoms

Stop managing this alone. Route directly to one of the following:

**Nigeria 🇳🇬**
- MANI (24/7 Mental Health Support): **0809 111 6264** or **0811 168 0686**
- SURPIN (Suicide Prevention): **+234 806 210 6493**
- Emergency Services: **112**

**United Kingdom 🇬🇧**
- Samaritans (24/7 Helpline): **116 123**
- NHS Mental Health Services: **111**
- Emergency Services: **999**

**United States & Canada 🇺🇸🇨🇦**
- National Crisis Lifeline: **988**
- Postpartum Support International (PSI): **1-800-944-4773** *(or text "HELP" to the same number)*
- Emergency Services: **911**

## For Partners Reading This Page

If you have noticed these signs in the mother — she cannot name them herself, or she is minimising them — you can name it. You can say:

> *"I've noticed something feels different. Not just tired. I want us to look at the safety checklist together. I'll come with you to speak to someone."*

That is the most important shift you can make.
