Before signing any roster, both adults must read and agree to the following guardrails. These are not optional. They are the safety boundary within which the entire system operates.

---

## Rule 1: Infant Safety is Paramount — Always

**Safe-Sleep Standards**
This contract controls adult coordination only. It does NOT override safe-sleep practices. The baby must always sleep on their back, on a firm, flat, separate sleep surface (crib or bassinet), free of blankets, pillows, bumper pads, or soft toys — in line with World Health Organization (WHO) and pediatric guidelines. If you are unsure what safe sleep looks like for your baby's age and stage, ask your pediatrician or midwife before implementing any roster.

**Vigilance Requirement**
The active Shift Manager must remain fully awake and alert during their shift while handling the baby. Never hold the baby in a bed, sofa, or armchair if there is any risk of falling asleep. If you feel unsafe from fatigue, activate the emergency handoff immediately (see Part 11).

**No Earplugs for the Active Shift Manager**
The parent who is on shift must NOT wear earplugs, headphones, or use any sleep-inducing aid. They must remain fully capable of hearing and responding to the baby. The off-duty parent may use earplugs or white noise to protect their sleep opportunity — that is the point of the system.

---

## Rule 2: Baby Concerns Always Override the Roster

If the baby cannot be settled, or the active caregiver is concerned about feeding, illness, injury, breathing, or any unusual behavior — the active caregiver assesses the baby and follows their existing pediatric or clinical guidance, or seeks appropriate help. Use the agreed phrase **"I need a handoff"** to coordinate partner support if needed. The roster pauses. Baby comes first.

---

## Rule 3: This Product is a Coordination Tool, Not Medical Advice

This guide provides household scheduling models and relationship communication tools. It does not contain clinical diagnoses, pediatric medical advice, or safe-sleep certifications. For specific feeding schedules, safe-sleep arrangements, or any physical concerns about the baby, consult your pediatrician, midwife, or certified lactation consultant.
