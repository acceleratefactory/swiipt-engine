# V06 — Part 2: The Read — "The Argument That Ends the War"
## Every Night, Just Me
### A postpartum night-coordination system that replaces nightly arguing with a pre-agreed roster, a fairness ledger, and the exact words to make it stick.

> **Product:** V06 | **Module:** 2 of 13 | **Type:** Short Read (2–3 pages)
> **Evidence label:** "Educational content informed by relationship research, postpartum health guidance, and lived experience. It is not a substitute for medical, clinical, or mental-health assessment."

---

When you are waking up every 90 minutes to a crying baby while your partner sleeps peacefully in the next room, the issue feels deeply personal. It feels like a lack of love, a lack of awareness, or a lack of care.

The next morning, the conversation usually goes like this:

> **Mother:** *"I need you to help more at night. I am dying here."*
> **Partner:** *"Just tell me what to do, I'll help. But you know I have work tomorrow."*

That night, the baby cries. The mother lies awake waiting for the partner to wake up. He doesn't. She becomes furious, nudges him aggressively, or simply gets up herself, muttering in resentment.

Here is the hard truth: **Unstructured nightly negotiation fails 100% of the time.**

It fails for three structural reasons:

**1. The Burden of Asking**
Asking your partner to "help" is actually cognitive labor. You have to wake up, evaluate if the cry is urgent, decide to wake him, and instruct him on what to do. By the time you have done all that, you are already fully awake and your sleep cycle is ruined.

**2. The "Helper" Reframe**
When a partner is positioned as a "helper," they wait for instructions. They do not take ownership. If the baby is wet, they wait to be told to change the diaper. If the baby won't settle, they hand the baby back because "they want you."

**3. Exhaustion Negotiation**
At 3:00 AM, both of your brains are operating at near-zero capacity. Arguing about "whose turn it is" under the influence of severe sleep deprivation is like trying to negotiate a contract during a mild concussion.

---

## The Solution: Shift-Based Adult Coordination

We must stop negotiating at night. We must externalize the schedule.

A written contract removes the emotional labor of asking. It converts a nightly confrontation into a pre-agreed default. Nobody has to ask — the schedule asks. The partner ceases to be a "helper" and becomes a **co-manager** who owns a specific, non-optional shift block.

This is not about blame. It is about building a system that works even when you are too exhausted to be generous with each other.
